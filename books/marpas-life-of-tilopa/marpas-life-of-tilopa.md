*Namo guru deva ḍākinī. Samayam anusmara. Siddhyantu ye.*[^invocation]

The three bodies[^three-bodies] are beyond elaboration[^beyond-elaboration] and wide as the sky. They ripen those to be trained[^to-be-trained] in the nine moods of the dance[^nine-moods]. Like a great lake, unstirred and a little clear, the three bodies have the ḍākinīs[^dakini] for their waves. From them came the lotus-born[^lotus-born], great bliss[^great-bliss], the fruit[^fruit] itself made manifest: Prajñābhadra[^prajnabhadra], protector of beings[^protector-of-beings]. To him I bow, and to the great paṇḍita[^pandita] Nāropa, who bore the twelve great hardships[^twelve-hardships].

> The jewel of the three bodies,\
> sealed against all who do not know it,\
> with the key, the ḍākinīs' secret word[^secret-word],\
> the three jewels[^three-jewels] are shown in full:\
> by the wish-fulfilling jewel[^wish-fulfilling-jewel] of introduction[^introduction]\
> the dharma body[^dharma-body] is pointed out;\
> by the wish-fulfilling jewel of dream[^dream]\
> the enjoyment body[^enjoyment-body] is made to dwell in the body;\
> by the wish-fulfilling jewel of the lineage[^lineage]\
> the dharma body is shown as it is.\
> The ḍākinīs' whispered lineage[^whispered-lineage], the clear light[^clear-light] of the sun,\
> dreads to hear Rāhu the titan[^titan] named;\
> yet for Dode's sake I have set it down in letters:\
> ḍākinīs, forgive me, and grant your blessing[^blessing].\
> The whispered lineage's instructions[^instruction] are not shown to all:\
> hungry, still one does not eat one's father's and mother's flesh;\
> for gain, still one does not sell a deadly poison;\
> for a great show, still one does not put one's own heart on display.

This wondrous whispered lineage of the ḍākinīs has three parts. They are the marks of the master who teaches, the marks of the disciple who is taught, and the nature of the Dharma[^dharma] that is taught. The first: a master of the lineage, with experience and realization[^experience-realization], compassion[^compassion] and the rest. The second: a disciple able to seek buddhahood[^buddhahood] in his own mind in this one life and this one body[^one-life], and the rest. The third, the nature of the Dharma that is taught, is in three. Outwardly it is the instruction of the emanation body[^emanation-body], which clears away false notions[^false-notions]: the wish-fulfilling jewel of the lineage. Inwardly it is the instruction of the enjoyment body, practiced in experience: the wish-fulfilling jewel of the path of ripening[^path-of-ripening]. Secretly[^secretly] it is the instruction of the dharma body, which points out: the wish-fulfilling jewel of the path of freedom[^path-of-freedom].

Of these, this is the instruction of the emanation body, the wish-fulfilling jewel of the lineage. It holds two accounts: the account of the wisdom ḍākinī[^wisdom-dakini] of the dharma body, and the account of the lineage of the accomplished ones[^accomplished-ones] of the emanation body. The first: as all the buddhas dwell in three bodies, so this wisdom ḍākinī dwells in three bodies, as is told at length below. The second is the lives of the lineage of the accomplished ones of the emanation body. It begins with the life of Tilo, which has four parts. First, how he was known as a man. Second, how he was known as an emanation[^emanation] of Cakrasaṃvara[^cakrasamvara]. Third, how he was known as Cakrasaṃvara himself. Fourth, how he was known as the body in which all the buddhas gather.

## 1 Tilopa as a man

First, taken as a man, the great lord Tilopa has four great qualities. First, in the provisional sense[^provisional], the ḍākinīs foretold him, and he sought a guru[^guru] and practiced. Second, he overwhelmed the ḍākinīs and asked them for the Dharma. Third, in the definitive sense[^definitive], he was shown to have no human guru. Fourth, he showed his manifold emanations.

## 1.1 The boy who herded buffalo

First, Tilopa. In the east of India, in the land of Zahor, was a city called Dzako. Three lived there: the father, the Brahmin[^brahmin] Salwa; the mother, the Brahmin Salden; and the sister, the Brahmin Saldrön. They had no son. They made offerings to every shrine, outer and inner, and prayed. Then a son was born. At that moment the whole east of India was filled with light, so he was named Salö, Clear Light. Then they asked a Brahmin who read the marks[^read-the-marks], and he said:

> Whether he is a god[^god], a nāga[^naga] or a yakṣa[^yaksa],\
> or a buddha, I cannot tell:\
> cherish this holy being and guard him.

As he said, they cherished him and guarded him. Once the father had gone out, and mother and son were alone. Something flickered. When they looked, there were many ugly women with the marks[^with-the-marks], puffing out their breath, limping and leaning on sticks. The mother thought, "Are they demons[^demon]? Will my boy die?" and the women said:

> Cherish him and guard him as you may,\
> there is no land on earth where none must die.

So they said. His mother asked, "Then what can we do that will help?" and they said again:

> Let the boy herd buffalo and learn his letters:\
> a ḍākinī's prophecy[^prophecy] will come.

With that, they say, they vanished without a trace.

He grew up, and they set him to herd the buffalo, as they had been told. While he was herding, the women from before came again and asked about his country, his father and mother and the rest. The boy said:

> My country is Dzako, in the east;\
> my father is Salwa, a Brahmin;\
> my mother is Salden, a Brahmin;\
> my sister is Saldrön, a Brahmin;\
> and I myself am Salö, a Brahmin.\
> This tree is a sal tree.[^sal]\
> I have learned my letters, and I practice the holy Dharma;\
> I herd the buffalo for our wealth.

When he said this, the women, as though they were angry, said, "Child, you do not know:

> your country is Oḍḍiyāna[^oddiyana], in the west;\
> your father is Cakrasaṃvara;\
> your mother is Vajravārāhī[^vajravarahi];\
> your sister is the ḍākinī Giver of Bliss;\
> you yourself are Pañcapana.[^pancapana]\
> Do not herd the beast, the buffalo:\
> in the forest of the tree of awakening[^tree-of-awakening]\
> herd always the buffalo of experience."

So they said. The boy said, "I do not know how to herd that," and they said, "Go to the charnel ground[^charnel-ground] of Sala Bhehara. A guru will show you." So they foretold.

In the southern lands there is a charnel ground called Sala Bhehara, blessed by Maheśvara[^mahesvara]. When the outer and inner ḍākinīs were turning the wheel of the feast[^feast] there, the Brahmin's boy came, and he asked Caryāpa[^caryapa] for instruction too.

The great master Lawapa[^lawapa] sang:

> At the crossroads of a great city\
> I slept for twelve years\
> and won the siddhi[^siddhi] of Mahāmudrā[^mahamudra].

When he said this, the boy asked him for luminosity[^luminosity].

He looked for Nāgārjuna[^nagarjuna]. In that charnel ground, in an outcaste's[^outcaste] hut, a yogin[^yogin] who kept the yogin's discipline[^discipline] sat meditating. "Nāgārjuna has gone to teach the Dharma to the king of the gandharvas[^gandharva]," he said. "I have been sent to take you into my care, good man." The boy offered him a maṇḍala[^mandala] and asked. The yogin called up before him the maṇḍala of glorious Guhyasamāja[^guhyasamaja], gave him its empowerment[^empowerment] and explained its tantra[^tantra]. The boy saw the nature of mind[^nature-of-mind].

In a temple there, where his uncle was reciting the Perfection of Wisdom in a Hundred Thousand Lines, a woman came and said, "Do you know what this means?" He said he did not. "Then I will explain it," she said, and she explained its meaning. She gave him the empowerments of Hevajra[^hevajra] and Cakrasaṃvara and explained their tantras. "This is what Lūhipa[^luhipa] meant," she said. "Meditate on it." He said, "My uncle keeps me at the Hundred Thousand and will not let me meditate." She said, "Cut the cord that binds the Hundred Thousand and throw it from the temple door into the water. Put on the guise of a madman and meditate. I will bless it, and nothing at all will befall the Hundred Thousand." He did as she said, and nothing at all befell the Hundred Thousand.[^hundred] "He has gone mad," they said, and they locked him up. There he trained in the two stages[^two-stages], the essence of all the paths that shine clearly in the undivided mind of the Sugatas[^sugata].

Then came a prophecy:

> In the east, in the land of Bengal,\
> in the market of Pañcapana,\
> become the servant of Bharima the harlot\
> and her household, and you will be made clean:\
> bring the conduct[^conduct] to its end, and win accomplishment[^accomplishment].

So it was foretold, and he went there. By night he did the work of bringing men in and seeing them out. By day he pounded sesame, so in the language of India he was known as Tilopa, and in Tibetan as Til Sungzhab, the Sesame Keeper.

Then he went to the charnel ground called Kereli, where he and Bharima delighted each other[^delighted] in the conduct of the secret mantra[^secret-mantra], and brought it to its end.

Then, back in that market, as he went on pounding sesame, he won the accomplishment that is close to the supreme Mahāmudrā. The people of the city saw him in different ways: some saw a blazing mass of fire, and some saw fire blazing in his bone ornaments[^bone-ornaments]. They asked him for instruction. He said, "Let the innate[^innate] that has arisen in my mind enter the hearts of you who follow me." At once they were freed[^freed].

Then the king of that country came riding an elephant with his court to honor them. The Brahmin's boy and Bharima, the two of them, sang a vajra song[^vajra-song] in a great voice like Brahmā's[^brahma]:

> Sesame oil, the essence that lies within it:\
> the dull may know that it is in the seed,\
> but if they do not know the links of dependent arising[^dependent-arising]\
> they cannot bring the essence, the oil, out.\
> Just so the innate, primordial wisdom[^primordial-wisdom],\
> though it is in the heart of every being,\
> cannot be realized unless the guru points it out.\
> When the sesame is pounded and the husk is cleared away,\
> the essence, the oil, comes out; just so,\
> relying on the guru, the meaning of suchness[^suchness]\
> is shown in signs[^signs], as the oil of the sesame is.\
> All things become one nature, not divided.\
> Kye ho[^kye-ho]! The meaning far off and hard to fathom\
> is clear right now. What a wonder!

When they had sung this, all who were gathered there were freed. Here is the meaning of the song, untied. There is nothing out of keeping with the meaning to give up, and no remedy[^remedy], wisdom, to bring about. There are no grounds and paths[^grounds-paths] to travel and no fruit to gain. Only as a convention[^convention], in signs, must the guru show it. That is what it means. Then they asked him who his guru was, and he said:

> I have human gurus:\
> Nāgārjuna, Caryāpa, Lawapa,\
> and Kalpa Zangmo; these are mine,\
> the gurus of the four transmissions[^four-transmissions].

So he said. From Nāgārjuna he asked for the illusory body[^illusory-body], from Caryāpa for dream, from Lawapa for luminosity, and from the ḍākinī for inner heat[^inner-heat]. These are known as the four great rivers of the whispered lineage.

## 1.2 The ḍākinīs brought under his power, and the jewels asked for

Second, how he brought the ḍākinīs under his power and asked them for the Dharma. Again the women came and said:

> The whispered lineage without letters\
> is kept by the stainless ḍākinīs:\
> receive the three wish-fulfilling jewels.

When they said this, he said, "I cannot receive them," and again the women said:

> At Gandhola in the land of Oḍḍiyāna\
> one foretold and true to the pledge[^pledge],\
> one with the fortune[^fortune] to take them, receives them.

When they said this, he asked what he must do to receive them. They told him, "Take a crystal ladder, a bridge of jewels and a pair of grass mice, and go to Oḍḍiyāna."[^ladder] His father found these without trouble, and he took them and went.

Now in the land of Oḍḍiyāna in the west, in the temple of Gandhola, dwells the one called the ḍākinī of wisdom, the dharma body. She dwells as a queen, in the state where reference[^reference] has ceased, in non-duality[^non-duality], dwelling in the manner of not dwelling. Beside her is the one called the ḍākinī of the five families[^five-families], the enjoyment body. In a palace of jewels she keeps the three precious wish-fulfilling jewels hidden, locked in with indestructible iron and sealed with seven seals. Ringed by a fortress, a moat and an iron wall, she dwells as a minister. Beside her is the emanation body, the ḍākinī of action[^dakini-of-action], the devourer. To those with faith and devotion[^devotion] she gives siddhis. Those without faith, and those whose pledges are broken, she cuts down and devours. She dwells as a gatekeeper. The Brahmin's boy went to the temple of Gandhola in the land of Oḍḍiyāna in the west. The emanation body, the ḍākinī of action, came at him with a voice to crack the earth, a voice like thunder in the sky:

> I am the ḍākinī of action, the emanation body:\
> I delight in human flesh and lust for blood.

So saying, she came at him, and the Brahmin's boy said:

> Many ḍākinīs, most terrible,\
> cannot stir the tip of one hair of mine.

So he said, and he overwhelmed them with the discipline of awareness[^discipline-of-awareness]. His body did not move, his speech did not falter, his mind did not shrink. He gazed at them, and the ḍākinīs swooned. When they came round from the swoon, they said:

> Kye ma[^kye-ma]! As the moth is destroyed by the lamp,\
> wanting to eat you, we are undone.\
> Holy one, do with us as you please.

So they said. Then the Brahmin's boy said, "Let me in," and the ḍākinīs said:

> We are like servants and have little power;\
> unless we have asked the ministers,\
> we are eaters of flesh and drinkers of blood:\
> holy one, take this to heart.

They handed him over to the ḍākinīs of the enjoyment body. These said, "Your discipline looks slight. We will set you free: go up." The Brahmin's boy laid the bridge of jewels across the moat and set the crystal ladder against the iron wall. He opened the gate with the pair of grass mice and went in, and the ministers said:

> Terrible in body, terrible in speech,\
> bearing terrible weapons as well,\
> we are the ḍākinīs of the five families, the enjoyment body:\
> we delight in flesh and lust for blood.

So they said, and again the Brahmin's boy said:

> Many ḍākinīs, most terrible,\
> cannot stir one pore of my body's hair.

So he said. He gazed at them, and they swooned. "Let me in," he said, and the ministers said:

> We are like ministers and have little power;\
> unless we have asked the queen herself,\
> we are cut down by her decree:\
> holy one, take this to heart.

So they said. They prayed to the ḍākinī of the dharma body, and the boy went in. The wisdom ḍākinī of the dharma body sat ringed by heroes[^heroes] past counting, in a row on her right, and heroines in a row on her left. The Brahmin's boy did not bow to her, and her retinue said:

> This one, before her who of all the buddhas\
> is the mother, the Blessed Lady[^blessed-lady],\
> shows no respect: why not crush him?

So saying, they set about destroying him, and then the ḍākinī of the dharma body spoke to her retinue:

> This one, of all the buddhas\
> the father, is Cakrasaṃvara:\
> though you rained vajras down from the sky,\
> how could you overcome him now?

So she said, and the retinue, as though they did not know the boy was Cakrasaṃvara, said, "Who sent you? Who are you? What do you want?" and the Brahmin's boy said:

> I am Pañcapana;\
> my sister, the Giver of Bliss, has sent me;\
> with view[^view], conduct, fruit and pledge\
> I have come to take the jewels of the three bodies.

When he said this, the retinue laughed him to scorn and said with one voice:

> The blind look and see no form,\
> the deaf listen and hear no sound,\
> the mute speak and no one grasps the meaning:\
> deceived by a demon, there is no truth in it.

To this the master answered:

> One whose faults are spent\
> has no cause to speak false words:\
> no demon, it is the ḍākinī, truly.

When he said this, the wisdom ḍākinī gave the signs of the precious jewel. Of her body she gave a painted card[^painted-card]; of her speech, a seed syllable[^seed-syllable]; of her mind, a hand emblem[^hand-emblem]. And the Brahmin's boy said:

> From the secret treasury of the body, appearance and emptiness[^emptiness],\
> I ask for the common wish-fulfilling jewel;\
> from the secret treasury of speech, beyond words,\
> I ask for the wish-fulfilling jewel of the pledge;\
> from the secret treasury of mind, free of thought[^free-of-thought],\
> I ask for the wish-fulfilling jewel of the way things are[^way-things-are].

When he said this, the wisdom ḍākinī said:

> For the common wish-fulfilling jewel\
> the foretold key of experience is needed:\
> unforetold, it will not open.\
> For the wish-fulfilling jewel of the pledge\
> the key of the profound lineage of the word is needed:\
> without the remedy, it will not open.\
> For the wish-fulfilling jewel of the way things are\
> the key of profound wisdom is needed:\
> without realization it will not open.

So she said, and the Brahmin's boy said:

> The ḍākinīs' secret word is sealed by the heart:\
> the lamp of wisdom that clears the dark of ignorance[^ignorance],\
> self-aware[^self-aware], self-arisen, self-illumined:\
> I have the foretold key of experience.\
> In mind itself[^mind-itself], which nowhere arises,\
> self-freed[^self-freed], the dharma body,\
> self-freed, it dawns as Mahāmudrā:\
> I have the key of the pledge, self-freed.\
> Taking no object to mind,\
> no mote of thought arising,\
> in mind itself, the nature of things[^nature-of-things], the dharma body,\
> I have the key of experience that sees.

So he said, and the wisdom ḍākinīs of the enjoyment body and the emanation body, both together, sang this song with one voice:

> Our one father, the Blessed One[^blessed-one],\
> Tilo, the buddha, protector of beings,\
> Cakrasaṃvara, great bliss:\
> to you we offer the three wish-fulfilling jewels.

So saying, they gave him the root tantra[^root-tantra] in fifty-one chapters with its explanatory tantras, and the whispered lineage. And from the mouth of the ḍākinī of wisdom came this: "To accomplish my body, strive in the stage of generation[^generation]. For my speech, strive in the essence[^essence]; for my mind, in the stage of completion[^completion], Mahāmudrā. Go to the monastery of the Crown Jewel, and take Nāro, Riri and Kasori into your care."

With that the chief lady vanished. He was given the name Tilo Prajñābhadra, and he said:

> Like a bird in the sky,\
> the bird of luminous mind takes wing:\
> Prajñābhadra goes, unhindered.

When he said this, the ḍākinīs of the enjoyment body and the emanation body said:

> Holy one, what are you like?\
> We beg you to stay for our sake.

When they asked this, Tilo said:

> As the chief lady herself foretold,\
> for the sake of worthy vessels[^worthy-vessels], I, the yogin,\
> go to the monastery of the Crown Jewel.

So he said, and on the road as he went, the nine cycles[^nine-cycles] of the teaching of the bodiless ḍākinīs were given him out of the sky:

> The vajra speech of the bodiless ḍākinīs,\
> like the sweet songs of the gandharvas,\
> was heard thus from the empty sky:\
> Ripening and freeing, undo the knot of mind.\
> The pledge: look into the mirror of your own mind.\
> Turn the wheel, the lattice of channels and winds[^channels-winds].\
> Hold the precious jewel of great bliss, of speech.\
> Look at the lamp of awareness[^awareness], of wisdom.\
> Look at self-freed Mahāmudrā.\
> The sacred substance[^sacred-substance]: the sun of realization, the key.\
> Conduct: strike the water with a sword.\
> One taste[^one-taste]: look into the outer mirror.

He heard these from the sky, and Tilo said:

> As the chief lady showed the way,\
> in Gandhola, the illusory body,\
> I have taken in the secret of the bodiless ḍākinīs\
> and locked it with speech beyond words:\
> the bird of luminous mind flies off.

So saying, he went to the temple of Aḍona, the monastery of the Crown Jewel.

## 1.3 No human guru

Third, that he had no human guru. Many people gathered there, and since he had gained accomplishment and received the Dharma at Gandhola, they asked him who his guru was:

> I have no human guru:\
> my guru is the All-Knowing[^all-knowing].

So he said.

## 1.4 The eight tamings

Fourth, how he showed his manifold emanations. There are eight. He overwhelmed the yogin. He tamed the heretic[^heretic], the magician, the beer-seller, the singer, the butcher, the one who says acts bear no fruit[^acts-no-fruit], and the great sorcerer.[^order]

### 1.4.1 The yogin

First. In the south of India there was a king who loved his mother dearly and did whatever pleased her. "What good deed would please you?" he asked her. "I will do it." His mother said, "Gather paṇḍitas, siddhas[^siddha] and ḍākinīs. Raise a maṇḍala of jewels in the sky, and hold a great empowerment and a feast. That would make me glad." So he sent a runner to each of the paṇḍitas who could do it, and one runner to the siddhas. He invited the paṇḍitas and the siddhas. The paṇḍitas performed the rite of the ground[^rite-of-the-ground] and the rest, and it was proclaimed that whatever these did would succeed. Then an ugly woman with the marks came before the kusālis[^kusali] and said, "Who will be the master of your feast?" The Unrivaled said, "I will." "You will not do," she said. "My brother will." When they asked where he was, she said, "In the charnel ground." "Then go and fetch him," they said, and she went to fetch him and said that he would come. At the very end he came, and he sat with the Unrivaled on two thrones. They debated the logic that shows the contradictions and connections of word and meaning, and they were equal. They drew maṇḍalas in the sky and took turns making them vanish with rain and wind, and they were equal. They rode lions in a race to the sun and the moon, and again they were equal. Then Tilo brought the sun and the moon down to the ground and rode a lion on them. He turned his body inside out. On each hair he called up a maṇḍala with its charnel ground. In each charnel ground was a tree, and in each tree he sat cross-legged at play. The Unrivaled could not do it. All were amazed. "E ma[^e-ma]! From what does such magic come?

> From what does such wondrous conduct come?"

When they said this, he answered in song:

> Kye ma! When I sit, I sit in the midst of the sky;\
> when I lie down, I lie on the point of a spear;\
> when I look, I look into the heart of the sun and moon.\
> I am Tilopa, who has realized the ultimate[^ultimate]:\
> I am free of all striving.

So he said, and when he spoke of the nature beyond thought[^beyond-thought], all were freed. The yogin was given the name Mighty Intellect, and even now he lives in Oḍḍiyāna without passing away[^without-passing-away].

### 1.4.2 The heretic

Second, the taming of the heretic. At glorious Śrī Nālandā[^nalanda] there was a heretic teacher, a siddha. All, outsiders and insiders[^outsiders-insiders], had always to rise before him. Whoever did not had to vie with him in power and in debate. Tilopa did not rise, and he said, "You seem to think yourself something in power and in debate. Let us vie." They set the king as judge. All the paṇḍitas, outsiders and insiders, gathered on the terms that whoever lost would enter the teaching of the one who won. In debate the heretic was defeated. Vying in power, he could not prevail against Tilopa either, and the heretic sent fire out of his mouth and said:

> I who shake all three realms[^three-realms],\
> who is there that does not fear me?

When he said this, Tilopa turned it back and answered in song:

> If you would look, look with a blind man's eyes;\
> if you thirst, drink the water of a mirage;\
> if you sink, fill the vase[^vase] with wind:\
> Tilo is free of every fear.

So he said, and when he spoke of the nature of things beyond thought, all were freed. The heretic was given the name Black Virtue, and even now he lives in the Cool Grove[^cool-grove] without passing away.

### 1.4.3 The magician

Third, the taming of the magician. A magician was taking the king's country with an army of illusion. An ugly woman with the marks came and asked the king, "Who will be your general?" "This one and that one," he said. "They will not do," she said. "My brother will." "Where is your brother?" "In the charnel ground, on a sal tree a league high, he has tied a horse's tail. To it he has tied a corpse's legs, and to its arms five horses. He holds on to them and dances." So she said. When they told the king, he said, "That woman has spoken of three things that cannot be. Bring her here," and when they asked her, she said just what she had said before. He sent men to look, and it was as she had said. Then they invited Tilopa. Not knowing the army for an illusion, they were on the point of being routed, when he broke the illusion, killed some, and seized the magician. The magician said:

> You, a man who relies on the Dharma,\
> how can it be right to kill like that?

When he said this, Tilopa answered in song:

> In killing an army of illusion,\
> since it has no mind, there is no sin[^sin].\
> If you would kill, kill the man of illusion[^man-of-illusion];\
> if you would meditate, meditate as one without breath;\
> if you would speak, speak with a mute's tongue;\
> if you would desire, take the letters to their end.

So he said, and when he spoke of things beyond thought, all were freed. The magician was given the name Deceiver Who Tells the Truth, and even now he lives at Aṭṭahāsa[^attahasa] without passing away.[^magician]

### 1.4.4 The beer-seller

Fourth, the taming of the beer-seller. Once, when a woman who sold beer was selling her beer, a monkey and a cat that Tilo had sent forth drank her beer on credit, and she wept. People asked what was wrong, and she told them the tale. They said, "Pray to that yogin himself," and the beer-seller wept and said:

> My living is cut off:\
> in your compassion, take me into your care.

When she said this, in an instant he filled every jar of beer with beer, and sang a song:

> If you thirst, drink the boiling water of poison;\
> if you stray, chase the monkey and kill it;\
> for a remedy, rely on the great meditator, the cat;\
> turn everything into the one taste of the innate.\
> I am Tilopa, beggar of the ultimate,\
> a yogin you have never seen.

So he said, and when he spoke of great bliss beyond thought, all were freed. The beer-seller was given the name Sunlight Lamp, and even now she lives on the island of Sosa without passing away.[^ifyou]

### 1.4.5 The singer

Fifth, the taming of the singer. There was a skilled singer. Before him Tilopa sang a song of his own making. "Will you compete?" the singer asked. "Let us compete," he said, and the singer said, "Master, take up a song." "I will sing after," said Tilopa. The singer sang his song through to its end. Then the master sang, and his song had no end. The singer could not match him. He said:

> I am a singer who puts even\
> the world of Brahmā to shame.

When he said this, Tilopa answered in song:

> If you grieve, look at the city of the gandharvas;\
> if you listen, listen to the song of the bees;\
> if you look, look with a blind man's eyes:\
> all that is heard is like an echo.

So he said, and when he spoke of music beyond thought, all were freed. The singer was given the name Melodious Mute, and even now he lives in Nagara without passing away.[^singer]

### 1.4.6 The butcher

Sixth, the taming of the butcher. A butcher, who had killed all the young of living beings, was raising one son of his own. He cooked the meat and opened the carcass, meaning to give it to his son. Then Tilo made it appear to him as his own son's legs and arms. The butcher said:

> The sin I have done myself,\
> has it come round on me, or what is this?

He was tormented with grief, and Tilopa said, "Then if you kill no others, your own son will come back." "I will not kill," he said, and Tilopa answered in song:

> If you would open, open the heart of the clear oil;\
> if you would burn, burn with the fire at the end of time[^fire-end-of-time];\
> if you would draw out, draw the wind-mind[^wind-mind] out through the crown[^crown];\
> if you would wash, wash away the stains of mind.

So he said, and when he spoke of the practice of killing beyond thought, all were freed. The butcher was given the name Bliss-Making Joy, and even now he lives on the island of the rākṣasas[^raksasa] without passing away.[^butcher]

### 1.4.7 The one who said acts bear no fruit

Seventh, the taming of the one who held that acts bear no fruit. A Cārvāka[^carvaka], who denied cause and fruit, said there is neither virtue[^virtue] nor sin. The Buddhists said there is, and they made the master the judge of their debate. The master said, "Those who hold that acts have causes and fruits win." The Cārvāka said, "No one has seen it with his eyes." So the master led him off and showed him, by emanation, the gods and the hells[^hell] and all the rest. In one heaven there was a goddess without a mate. When he asked what she was, they said, "Some heretic is doing virtue; she is to be his companion." Then he led him to hell, where something was boiling in every copper cauldron. One had nothing in it. When he asked what was to be boiled there, they said, "All the heretics who say there is neither virtue nor sin are boiled here." He was terrified, they say. He said:

> By the sin heaped up by acts,\
> hell circles in one's own mind;\
> by the virtue heaped up by acts,\
> the higher realms[^higher-realms] circle in one's own mind.

When he said this, the master answered in song:

> If you cling, go off to the island of charnel grounds;\
> if you are low, turn to the top of the victory banner[^victory-banner].\
> Discursive thought[^discursive-thought] is the emanation body:\
> I have nothing at all to teach.

So he said, and when he spoke of the manifold beyond thought, all were freed. The Cārvāka was given the name Jinabodhi, and even now he lives on Śrīparvata[^sriparvata] without passing away.

Besides these, he taught the Dharma to sinners by the bearing of his body. To a craftsman he was a craftsman skilled in craft, to a meditator one accomplished. So he taught fishermen, dog keepers and others without number. They told him that they were fishermen and the like, and when they had told him, they said:

> Tilopa, who has realized the ultimate,\
> for you there is not even a name for virtue and sin.

So they said, and they asked him for instruction.

### 1.4.8 The sorcerer

Eighth, the taming of the sorcerer. A sorcerer had been killing everyone with his black magic, and Tilo saw that the time had come to tame him. The two vied in cutting down, and they were equal. But of all who died, Tilo took up the dead; some, the women and others, he did not take. He made the sorcerer himself like an old tree stump, and said, "Now, if I raise your women up, will you give up your magic?" The sorcerer could not bear it and said:[^sorcerer]

> This conduct, from which no one recovers,\
> is it any different from a butcher's?

When he said this, Tilopa answered in song:

> Look, look into your own mind:\
> swallow the mountain of appearances in one gulp;\
> drink the great ocean down in one draught;\
> cut the tether of saṃsāra[^samsara] and let it lie.

So he said, and when he spoke of activity beyond thought, all were freed. The sorcerer was given the name Nyiimi, and even now he lives at Kimitsikili.

## 2 An emanation of Cakrasaṃvara

The second general part: how he was shown to be an emanation of Cakrasaṃvara. In the east of India, on the bank of the Dukata, is the source of the river Khasu. There, in the charnel ground of the Masha Grove, is a monastery called Sorrowless. There Tilopa's uncle and his mother, who was a nun, were his abbot and master[^abbot-master], and there he was ordained as a monk and named Kalapa. The others gave themselves to the three wheels[^three-wheels] of a monk's work, but he did not keep the Dharma. He killed many small birds, and showed his head one way and his body another. Everyone mocked him. Now those in charge of discipline took counsel. The provost[^provost], with the followers of the Dharma in general and the monks in particular, called an assembly of the Sorrowless monastery and brought him to account. The king of that country said:[^birds]

> You, in a monk's form, killing living things:\
> what is your country, who are your abbot and master?

When he asked this, Tilopa answered in song:

> Sorrowless is my monastery;\
> my abbot and master are my mother and my uncle;\
> I myself am Tilo, the monk.\
> For ten million kalpas[^kalpa] I\
> have gone to a hundred buddha fields[^buddha-fields];\
> with Nāgārjuna and Āryadeva[^aryadeva]\
> and with the buddhas I have talked;\
> I have seen a thousand buddhas[^thousand-buddhas];\
> I have killed no living thing.

When he said this, the birds, they say, flew up with a whir and were gone. All believed, and he was known as an emanation of Cakrasaṃvara.

## 3 Cakrasaṃvara himself

Third, how he was held to be Cakrasaṃvara himself. He went begging for alms in the east of India. Going out, he set his feet down slowly, his eyes looked no farther than a yoke's length[^yoke] ahead, and he recited verses of poetry. Coming back, he spoke the blessings of giving and receiving[^giving-receiving], and the people grew devoted to him. The king of that country invited them all and honored them. Then the king said, "Let each of you bring forth a verse on giving and receiving. It must not go against the grammar of the grammarians, nor against logic, scripture, instruction, experience, or the realization of those who have realized." They all did so, and each agreed with one side or two. When his turn came, the master spoke. His verse went against none of them and reached the final meaning of each, and the king wrote it down:

> I have no father or mother:\
> Cakrasaṃvara, the supreme of bliss.\
> I have no abbot or master:\
> I am a self-arisen buddha.\
> I have no grammar or logic:\
> reasoning breaks out of awareness by itself.\
> Saṃvara's body, speech and mind\
> and my body, speech and mind are not divided:\
> I go into great bliss.

So he said, and he was known as Cakrasaṃvara himself.

## 4 The body of all the buddhas

Fourth, how he was shown to be the body in which all the buddhas of the three times[^three-times] gather. King Siṃhacandra invited many siddhas, honored them, and asked for empowerment. Tilopa raised a maṇḍala of colored powders[^colored-powders] in the sky, and each saw it differently. Tilo sang a song:

> My body is Hevajra,\
> my speech is Mahāmāyā[^mahamaya],\
> my mind is Cakrasaṃvara,\
> my aggregates and elements[^aggregates-elements], Guhyasamāja,\
> my limbs, the Black One, the tantra of sound,\
> my lesser limbs, Vajrabhairava[^vajrabhairava],\
> my body's hairs, the buddhas of the three times.

So he said, and Tilopa was known as the body in which all the buddhas gather.

By such deeds, at the end, he set the beings of Zahor in the east on the path of ripening and freedom. A city of seven hundred thousand was emptied.

So ends the life of Tilopa, lord of yogins, whom even the accomplished know as an emanation body. It is the account of what he did by his own great qualities, told as it is and told in full.

Written down at the monastery of glorious Drowolung for my son Dode. It is complete. Maṅgalam[^mangalam].[^colophon]

## Notes

[^invocation]: The invocation is in Sanskrit, as the Tibetan writes it: "Homage to the guru[^guru], the deity, the ḍākinī[^dakini]. Keep the pledge[^pledge] (*samaya*) in mind. May they be accomplished." See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud*, fol. 1b; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Samaya](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Samaya)."

[^three-bodies]: The three bodies (Sanskrit *trikāya*, Tibetan *sku gsum*) are the three ways a buddha is present: the dharma body[^dharma-body] (*dharmakāya*), awakened mind itself[^mind-itself], empty and without form; the enjoyment body[^enjoyment-body] (*saṃbhogakāya*), a body of light seen only by beings of great realization; and the emanation body[^emanation-body] (*nirmāṇakāya*), the form in which a buddha appears in the world. They are also three sides of the nature of mind[^nature-of-mind] in every being: everything that appears is emanation body, its radiance enjoyment body, its empty truth dharma body. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[sku gsum](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sku_gsum)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 91; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Three kayas](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Three_kayas)."

[^beyond-elaboration]: Beyond elaboration (Sanskrit *niṣprapañca*, Tibetan *spros bral*): free of everything the mind builds about the nature of things[^nature-of-things], such as existing and not existing. It describes the dharma body[^dharma-body] and emptiness[^emptiness]; in Mahāmudrā[^mahamudra] it is also the second of the four yogas, between one-pointedness and one taste. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[spros bral](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/spros_bral)," "[rnal 'byor bzhi](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rnal_'byor_bzhi)."

[^to-be-trained]: Those to be trained (*gdul bya*), or tamed, are the beings a buddha or a master can bring to awakening, those a teaching is meant for; to ripen them is the work of the three bodies[^three-bodies]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[gdul bya](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gdul_bya)."

[^nine-moods]: The nine moods of the dance (*gar gyi nyams dgu*) are the nine moods the wrathful deities of the tantras[^tantra] show: charming, heroic and repulsive in body; laughing, fierce and terrifying in speech; compassionate, majestic and peaceful in mind. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[gar gyi nyams dgu](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gar_gyi_nyams_dgu)."

[^dakini]: A ḍākinī (Tibetan *mkha' 'gro ma*, "she who goes in the sky") is a female embodiment of enlightened energy: at the highest a wisdom ḍākinī[^wisdom-dakini], a fully awakened buddha in female form; below that, worldly ḍākinīs, spirits who carry out tasks and eat flesh. The ḍākinīs guard the secret teachings and give them only to those fit to hold them, and they are one of the Three Roots, with the guru[^guru] and the deity. In this life they foretell Tilopa, test him and give him the whispered lineage[^whispered-lineage]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[mkha' 'gro ma](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/mkha'_'gro_ma)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Dakini](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Dakini)."

[^lotus-born]: "The lotus-born" is *chu skyes*, "born of water", a poet's word for the lotus: as a lotus rises from a lake, Prajñābhadra[^prajnabhadra], who is Tilopa, rises from the lake of the three bodies[^three-bodies]. It is not here the name of Padmasambhava. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[chu skyes](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/chu_skyes)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 414.

[^great-bliss]: Great bliss (Sanskrit *mahāsukha*, Tibetan *bde ba chen po*) is the bliss of awakening as the tantras[^tantra] teach it, never apart from emptiness[^emptiness]. It is also a name of Cakrasaṃvara[^cakrasamvara], whose Tibetan name, *bde mchog*, means "supreme bliss". See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[bde ba chen po](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bde_ba_chen_po)," "['khor lo bde mchog](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'khor_lo_bde_mchog)."

[^fruit]: The fruit (*'bras bu*) is the result of the path, awakening itself; "the fruit made manifest" calls Tilopa awakening in person. The same word is the result an act brings, as in "acts bear no fruit[^acts-no-fruit]", and with view[^view], conduct[^conduct] and pledge[^pledge] it is one of the four things a practitioner holds. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "['bras bu](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'bras_bu)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 929.

[^prajnabhadra]: Prajñābhadra, "Excellent Wisdom" (Tibetan *shes rab bzang po*), is Tilopa, by the name the wisdom ḍākinī[^wisdom-dakini] gives him in Oḍḍiyāna[^oddiyana] in 1.2. See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud*, fol. 7a.

[^protector-of-beings]: "Protector of beings" (*'gro ba'i mgon po*) is a title of a buddha, or of a master seen as one. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "['gro ba'i mgon po](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'gro_ba'i_mgon_po)."

[^pandita]: A paṇḍita (Tibetan *mkhas pa*, "learned one") is a scholar of the Buddhist teachings and the sciences of India, such as the great teachers of its monastic universities. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[mkhas pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/mkhas_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 183.

[^twelve-hardships]: Nāropa, a great scholar of the monastic universities of India who left his seat to seek Tilopa, was Tilopa's chief disciple and Marpa's own guru[^guru]. The twelve great hardships (*dka' ba bcu gnyis*) are the twelve trials Tilopa put him through before he gave him the teachings. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[dka' ba bcu gnyis](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dka'_ba_bcu_gnyis)," "[nā ro pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/nA_ro_pa)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Naropa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naropa)."

[^secret-word]: The ḍākinīs'[^dakini] secret word (*mkha' 'gro'i gsang tshig*) is their hidden, symbolic speech, the code in which the secret teachings are kept, so that only one who holds its key can open them. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[gsang tshig](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gsang_tshig)."

[^three-jewels]: The three jewels here are the three wish-fulfilling jewels[^wish-fulfilling-jewel] of this cycle of teaching, named in the lines that follow: introduction[^introduction], dream[^dream] and the lineage[^lineage]. They are not the Three Jewels of all Buddhists, the Buddha, the Dharma[^dharma] and the Saṅgha. See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud*, fol. 1b.

[^wish-fulfilling-jewel]: A wish-fulfilling jewel (Sanskrit *cintāmaṇi*, Tibetan *yid bzhin nor bu*) is the gem of Indian legend that grants every wish; the Buddha, the guru[^guru] and the nature of mind[^nature-of-mind] are all called by its name. The teachings of the ḍākinīs'[^dakini] whispered lineage[^whispered-lineage] are kept as three such jewels, and this life belongs to the first, the jewel of the lineage[^lineage]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[yid bzhin nor bu](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/yid_bzhin_nor_bu)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Cintamani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cintamani)."

[^introduction]: Introduction (*ngo sprod*) is the pointing-out instruction[^instruction]: the guru[^guru] shows the disciple the nature of his own mind directly, as one introduces a person face to face. The guru who gives it is the disciple's root guru. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[ngo sprod](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/ngo_sprod)."

[^dharma-body]: The dharma body (Sanskrit *dharmakāya*, Tibetan *chos sku*) is the first of the three bodies[^three-bodies]: the empty, unconditioned truth of awakened mind, which no concept has ever entered. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[chos sku](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/chos_sku)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Dharmakaya](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Dharmakaya)."

[^dream]: Dream (*rmi lam*) here is the yoga of dream, knowing that one dreams and training the mind in the dream. It is one of the six doctrines of Nāropa (*na ro chos drug*), often called the six yogas: inner heat[^inner-heat], the illusory body[^illusory-body], dream, luminosity[^luminosity], transference and the intermediate state. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[rmi lam](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rmi_lam)," "[na ro chos drug](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/na_ro_chos_drug)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Six Yogas of Naropa](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Six_Yogas_of_Naropa)."

[^enjoyment-body]: The enjoyment body (Sanskrit *saṃbhogakāya*, Tibetan *longs sku*) is the second of the three bodies[^three-bodies]: a form body of light, adorned with the major and minor marks, which appears only to bodhisattvas and from which the emanation body[^emanation-body] arises. In the palace of 1.2 it is the rank of the ḍākinīs[^dakini] of the five families[^five-families]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[longs spyod rdzogs pa'i sku](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/longs_spyod_rdzogs_pa'i_sku)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Sambhogakaya](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Sambhogakaya)."

[^lineage]: A lineage (*brgyud pa*) is the unbroken line through which a teaching passes, each holder receiving it from the one before. "The jewel of the lineage" is the account of that line, beginning with Tilopa's life. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[brgyud pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/brgyud_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 342.

[^whispered-lineage]: The whispered lineage (*snyan brgyud*, "ear lineage[^lineage]") is teaching passed only by word of mouth, from a master's lips to a disciple's ear. This one, the hearing lineage of Cakrasaṃvara[^cakrasamvara], came from the ḍākinīs[^dakini] to Tilopa, and through Nāropa and Marpa to Milarepa and Rechungpa. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[snyan brgyud](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/snyan_brgyud)," "[bde mchog snyan brgyud](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bde_mchog_snyan_brgyud)."

[^clear-light]: Clear light (*'od gsal*), called luminosity[^luminosity] in the rest of the book, is the bright, knowing nature of mind[^nature-of-mind]; the whispered lineage[^whispered-lineage] is that light, as the sun is light. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "['od gsal](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'od_gsal)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 1120.

[^titan]: Rāhu is a titan (Sanskrit *asura*, Tibetan *lha min*, "not a god[^god]"), one of the jealous demigods who live inside Mount Meru and wage war on the gods. In Indian myth he swallows the sun and the moon, which is why they are eclipsed; the whispered lineage[^whispered-lineage], the light of the sun, dreads him as the sun dreads eclipse. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[lha min](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/lha_min)," "[sgra gcan](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sgra_gcan)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 331; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Demigods](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Demigods)."

[^blessing]: Blessing (Sanskrit *adhiṣṭhāna*, Tibetan *byin rlabs*) is the power of the buddhas, a guru[^guru] or the ḍākinīs[^dakini] passed into a practitioner, a stream of energy that makes realization ripen; to bless is to give it. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[byin rlabs](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/byin_rlabs)."

[^instruction]: Instructions (*gdams ngag*) are a master's own practical teaching on how to practice, passed by word of mouth, as against the scriptures themselves. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[gdams ngag](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gdams_ngag)."

[^dharma]: The Dharma (Sanskrit *dharma*, Tibetan *chos*) is the Buddha's teaching and the truth it teaches; "the holy Dharma" (*dam chos*, *saddharma*) is the same. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dam chos](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dam_chos)."

[^experience-realization]: Experience and realization (*nyams rtogs*): experience is the passing states of meditation, such as bliss, clarity and stillness; realization is unchanging understanding of the nature of things[^nature-of-things]. A master of the lineage[^lineage] has both. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[nyams rtogs](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/nyams_rtogs)."

[^compassion]: Compassion (Sanskrit *karuṇā*, Tibetan *thugs rje*) is the wish that beings be free of suffering, and the ever-responsive energy with which an awakened mind acts for them. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[thugs rje](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/thugs_rje)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 579.

[^buddhahood]: A buddha (Tibetan *sangs rgyas*) is one fully awakened from the sleep of ignorance[^ignorance]; buddhahood is that state. The Buddha of history is one buddha among countless buddhas. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[sangs rgyas](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sangs_rgyas)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 1265.

[^one-life]: "In this one life and this one body" (*tshe gcig lus gcig*) is the promise of the tantras[^tantra], that buddhahood[^buddhahood] can be won within a single lifetime. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[tshe gcig lus gcig](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/tshe_gcig_lus_gcig)."

[^emanation-body]: The emanation body (Sanskrit *nirmāṇakāya*, Tibetan *sprul sku*) is the third of the three bodies[^three-bodies]: a form body that appears to beings both pure and impure, to tame them, as a buddha, a craftsman, a musician or anything that will help. Tilopa's life is told as the life of an emanation body; in Tibet the same word names an incarnate lama, a tulku. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[sprul sku](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sprul_sku)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 812; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Nirmanakaya](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Nirmanakaya)."

[^false-notions]: False notions (*sgro 'dogs*) are the ideas the mind lays over things that are not in them, the superimpositions, and the doubts that come with them; an instruction[^instruction] that clears them away does it by hearing and reflection. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[sgro 'dogs](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sgro_'dogs)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 337.

[^path-of-ripening]: The path of ripening (*smin lam*) is the four empowerments[^empowerment], which ripen a disciple's being with the capacity to realize the bodies of a buddha. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[smin lam](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/smin_lam)," "[smin grol](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/smin_grol)."

[^secretly]: Outer, inner and secret (*phyi nang gsang gsum*) are the three levels at which the teachings are given, each deeper and more hidden than the last; here they match the three bodies[^three-bodies], from the emanation body[^emanation-body] outside to the dharma body[^dharma-body] within. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[phyi nang gsang gsum](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/phyi_nang_gsang_gsum)."

[^path-of-freedom]: The path of freedom (*grol lam*) is the practice, after empowerment[^empowerment], of the guru's[^guru] oral instructions[^instruction], and above all of resting in the natural state of mind, Mahāmudrā[^mahamudra]. Together the two paths are "ripening and freeing" (*smin grol*). See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[grol lam](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/grol_lam)," "[smin grol](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/smin_grol)."

[^wisdom-dakini]: The wisdom ḍākinī (Sanskrit *jñānaḍākinī*, Tibetan *ye shes mkha' 'gro ma*) is a fully awakened being in a pure female form, as against the worldly ḍākinīs[^dakini], who are spirits. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[ye shes mkha' 'gro ma](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/ye_shes_mkha'_'gro_ma)."

[^accomplished-ones]: The accomplished ones (Sanskrit *siddha*, Tibetan *grub thob*) are those who have won siddhi[^siddhi], the supreme or the ordinary accomplishments[^accomplishment], by the practice of the tantras[^tantra]; the greatest of them are the mahāsiddhas, and Tilopa is counted among the eighty-four. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[grub thob](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/grub_thob)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Tilopa](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Tilopa)."

[^emanation]: An emanation (Sanskrit *nirmita*, Tibetan *sprul pa*) is a form an awakened being sends out or takes on by its power, a body, a person or a whole scene; "an emanation of Cakrasaṃvara[^cakrasamvara]" is that deity appearing as Tilopa. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[sprul pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sprul_pa)."

[^cakrasamvara]: Cakrasaṃvara, "Wheel of Supreme Bliss" (Tibetan *'khor lo bde mchog*), is a chief deity of the highest yoga tantras[^tantra] of the new schools, and of their mother tantras: a wrathful deity, usually blue, with four faces and twelve arms, in union with his consort Vajravārāhī[^vajravarahi]. The whispered lineage[^whispered-lineage] is his. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "['khor lo bde mchog](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'khor_lo_bde_mchog)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 669; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Chakrasamvara](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Chakrasamvara)."

[^provisional]: In the provisional sense (Sanskrit *neyārtha*, Tibetan *drang don*): a meaning that leads, one that needs interpreting; here, Tilopa's life told as though he were an ordinary man who needed gurus[^guru]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[drang don](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/drang_don)."

[^guru]: A guru (Tibetan *bla ma*, lama) is a spiritual teacher; in the tantras[^tantra], the vajra master who gives empowerment[^empowerment], keeps the pledges[^pledge] and has experience and realization[^experience-realization], and is regarded as a buddha. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[bla ma](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bla_ma)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 900.

[^definitive]: In the definitive sense (Sanskrit *nītārtha*, Tibetan *nges don*): the real, final meaning; here, that Tilopa, being a buddha, had no human guru[^guru]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[nges don](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/nges_don)."

[^brahmin]: A Brahmin (Sanskrit *brāhmaṇa*, Tibetan *bram ze*) is a member of the priestly class of Indian society, the highest of the four classes, the keepers of the rites. Tilopa is born into a Brahmin family. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[bram ze](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bram_ze)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 896; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin)."

[^read-the-marks]: A reader of marks (*mtshan mkhan*) is one versed in the art of reading signs[^signs] and omens, here in a newborn child's body; the Tibetan names him a Brahmin[^brahmin] reader of marks. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[mtshan mkhan](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/mtshan_mkhan)."

[^god]: A god (Sanskrit *deva*, Tibetan *lha*) is a being of the heavens: long-lived and happy but still mortal and still in saṃsāra[^samsara], and no creator. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[lha](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/lha)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 1331.

[^naga]: A nāga (Tibetan *klu*) is a serpent spirit of springs, lakes and the underworld, rich and magical, and the cause of certain illnesses when offended. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[klu](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/klu)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 45; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Naga](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Naga)."

[^yaksa]: A yakṣa (Tibetan *gnod sbyin*, "harm-bringer") is a powerful spirit of wealth and wild places, one of the eight classes of gods[^god] and demons[^demon], now helping and now harming. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[gnod sbyin](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gnod_sbyin)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Yaksha](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Yaksha)."

[^with-the-marks]: "With the marks" (*mtshan ma can*): bearing the defining signs[^signs] of a ḍākinī[^dakini], here old age and ugliness, the breath and the limping gait; the women who come to the boy are ḍākinīs. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[mtshan ma can](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/mtshan_ma_can)."

[^demon]: Demons (*'dre*, *bdud*) are harmful spirits; the Māra (*bdud*) who tried to keep the Buddha from awakening stands, for a practitioner, for his own clinging to a self. One who does not know a ḍākinī[^dakini] may take her for a demon, as the retinue does in 1.2. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "['dre](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'dre)," "[bdud](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bdud)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 697.

[^prophecy]: A prophecy (*lung bstan*) is a foretelling, by a buddha, a deity or a ḍākinī[^dakini], that directs a practitioner where to go and what to do; Tilopa's life moves from one prophecy to the next. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[lung bstan](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/lung_bstan)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 1216.

[^sal]: The Tibetan names the boy's tree *sha ba*, a word the dictionaries know only as "deer"; the sal tree, sacred in India, is the likeliest tree meant. The same tree, "a league high", stands in the charnel ground[^charnel-ground] of 1.4.3. See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud*, fols. 4a, 8b; *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[sha ba](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sha_ba)."

[^oddiyana]: Oḍḍiyāna (Tibetan *u rgyan*), the "land of the ḍākinīs[^dakini]", was a real place northwest of ancient India, which many scholars put in the Swat valley of today's Pakistan, and which tradition made a pure land where the tantras[^tantra] flourish. Almost every great master of the tantras is said to have gone there, in body or in vision. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[u rgyan](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/u_rgyan)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Oddiyana](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Oddiyana)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Oddiyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oddiyana)."

[^vajravarahi]: Vajravārāhī, "Vajra Sow" (Tibetan *rdo rje phag mo*), is the consort of Cakrasaṃvara[^cakrasamvara] and the root of all the ḍākinīs'[^dakini] emanations[^emanation], a wisdom ḍākinī[^wisdom-dakini] shown red, with a sow's head beside her own. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rdo rje phag mo](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rdo_rje_phag_mo)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Vajravarahi](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Vajravarahi)."

[^pancapana]: The name is not explained. It is nearly the name of the market town in Bengal where, in the prophecy[^prophecy] that follows, Tilopa is sent to serve Bharima. See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud*, fols. 4a, 4b.

[^tree-of-awakening]: The tree of awakening (*byang chub shing*) is the bodhi tree, the fig at Bodh Gaya under which the Buddha awoke; here the place where the boy is to win his own awakening. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[byang chub shing](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/byang_chub_shing)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Bodhi tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodhi_tree)."

[^charnel-ground]: A charnel ground (Sanskrit *śmaśāna*, Tibetan *dur khrod*) is where the dead were left, burned or cut up for the birds. Terrifying and lonely, it is where the yogins[^yogin] of the tantras[^tantra] practiced, and it stands for the death of clinging to this body and life; India had eight great ones. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dur khrod](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dur_khrod)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 630; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.vv. "[Charnel ground](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Charnel_ground)," "[Eight great charnel grounds](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Eight_great_charnel_grounds)."

[^mahesvara]: Maheśvara, "the Great Lord" (Tibetan *dbang phyug chen po*), is the Hindu god[^god] Śiva; the eight great charnel grounds[^charnel-ground] are said to have grown up where the parts of Rudra's body fell. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dbang phyug chen po](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dbang_phyug_chen_po)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Eight great charnel grounds](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Eight_great_charnel_grounds)."

[^feast]: The wheel of the feast (Sanskrit *gaṇacakra*, Tibetan *tshogs kyi 'khor lo*) is a gathering of the tantras[^tantra] in which practitioners offer food and drink, meat, fish and wine among them, to the deities and enjoy them, to gather merit and mend their pledges[^pledge]. The "master of your feast" in 1.4.1 is the one who leads it. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[tshogs kyi 'khor lo](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/tshogs_kyi_'khor_lo)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Ganachakra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganachakra)."

[^caryapa]: Caryāpa, "the one of conduct[^conduct]", is a mahāsiddha of India; in this life he is one of Tilopa's four human gurus[^guru], who gave him dream[^dream]. See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud*, fol. 5a; *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[spyod pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/spyod_pa)."

[^lawapa]: Lawapa (Tibetan *lva ba pa*), or Kambalapāda, "the one with the blanket", is a mahāsiddha of the tenth century named for the woolen blanket he wore. One of Tilopa's four human gurus[^guru], he gave him luminosity[^luminosity]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[lva ba pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/lva_ba_pa)," "[la ba pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/la_ba_pa)."

[^siddhi]: A siddhi (Tibetan *dngos grub*), an accomplishment[^accomplishment], is what practice attains. The common siddhis are eight worldly powers, such as clairvoyance, flight and invisibility; the supreme siddhi is complete awakening, Mahāmudrā[^mahamudra]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dngos grub](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dngos_grub)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 359; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Siddhi](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Siddhi)."

[^mahamudra]: Mahāmudrā, "the great seal" (Tibetan *phyag rgya chen po*), is the nature of mind[^nature-of-mind], the seal set on all things in saṃsāra[^samsara], nirvāṇa and the path, and the most direct practice for realizing it; it is the meditation tradition of the Kagyü, passed from Nāropa to Marpa. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[phyag rgya chen po](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/phyag_rgya_chen_po)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 831; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Mahamudra](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Mahamudra)."

[^luminosity]: Luminosity (*'od gsal*), the clear light[^clear-light] of the opening song, is the knowing radiance of the nature of mind[^nature-of-mind]; as one of the six doctrines of Nāropa it is the practice of recognizing it, above all in deep sleep. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "['od gsal](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'od_gsal)," "[bka' babs bzhi](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bka'_babs_bzhi)."

[^nagarjuna]: Nāgārjuna (Tibetan *klu sgrub*), the great master of the second or third century who founded the Middle Way, is also counted among the eighty-four mahāsiddhas and stands in the Guhyasamāja[^guhyasamaja] tradition; here he is one of Tilopa's four human gurus[^guru], who gave him the illusory body[^illusory-body]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[klu sgrub](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/klu_sgrub)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Nagarjuna](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Nagarjuna)."

[^outcaste]: An outcaste (Sanskrit *mātaṅga*, Tibetan *gdol pa*) stood below the four classes of Indian society, the lowest and most despised; a yogin[^yogin] living in an outcaste's hut has set purity and impurity aside. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[ma tang ga](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/ma_tang_ga)," "[gdol pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gdol_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 663.

[^yogin]: A yogin (Tibetan *rnal 'byor pa*, "one who masters the real") is a practitioner of yoga; here a practitioner of the tantras[^tantra] who lives apart, an ascetic given to meditation. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rnal 'byor pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rnal_'byor_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 763.

[^discipline]: The yogin's discipline (Sanskrit *vrata*, Tibetan *brtul zhugs*) is the unconventional conduct[^conduct] advanced practitioners of the tantras[^tantra] take up, "vanquishing ordinary behaviour", to deepen their practice and train their awareness[^awareness], as Tilopa's madness does in 1.1. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[brtul zhugs](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/brtul_zhugs)."

[^gandharva]: A gandharva (Tibetan *dri za*, "scent eater") is a celestial musician who lives on scents, at the rim of Mount Meru; "the city of the gandharvas" is a mirage, castles in the air. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[dri za](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dri_za)," "[dri za'i grong khyer](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dri_za'i_grong_khyer)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Gandharva](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Gandharva)."

[^mandala]: A maṇḍala (Tibetan *dkyil 'khor*, "center and surrounding") is either of two things. The maṇḍala of a deity is its palace and circle of deities, drawn in colored sand or visualized, into which a disciple is led at empowerment[^empowerment]. The maṇḍala offered to a guru[^guru] is the whole universe, visualized and set out in heaps on a plate. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dkyil 'khor](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dkyil_'khor)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 56; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Mandala](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Mandala)."

[^guhyasamaja]: Guhyasamāja, "Assembly of Secrets" (Tibetan *gsang ba 'dus pa*), is one of the major tantras[^tantra] of the new schools and its deity, a form of the buddha Akṣobhya. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[gsang ba 'dus pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gsang_ba_'dus_pa)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Guhyasamaja](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Guhyasamaja)."

[^empowerment]: An empowerment (Sanskrit *abhiṣeka*, Tibetan *dbang*) is the rite of the Vajrayāna that awakens the capacity for primordial wisdom[^primordial-wisdom] in a disciple and gives leave to practice; "without empowerment there is no accomplishment[^accomplishment]." See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dbang bskur](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dbang_bskur)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Empowerment](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Empowerment)."

[^tantra]: A tantra (Tibetan *rgyud*, "continuity") is a scripture of the Vajrayāna; the Guhyasamāja[^guhyasamaja] Tantra defines it as the continuity of ground, path and fruit[^fruit]. To explain a tantra is to teach it. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rgyud](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rgyud)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 318.

[^nature-of-mind]: The nature of mind (*sems kyi ngo bo*) is what mind is in itself, beneath its thoughts; to see it is the aim of the pointing-out instruction[^instruction] and of Mahāmudrā[^mahamudra]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[sems kyi ngo bo](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sems_kyi_ngo_bo)," "[sems kyi rang bzhin](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sems_kyi_rang_bzhin)."

[^hevajra]: Hevajra (Tibetan *kye rdo rje*) is a chief deity of the highest yoga tantra[^tantra] and the tantra named after him, much practiced by the new schools. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[kye rdo rje](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/kye_rdo_rje)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Hevajra](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Hevajra)."

[^luhipa]: Lūhipa, or Lūipa, "the Fish-Gut Eater", a mahāsiddha of the tenth century born a prince, is the first of the eighty-four mahāsiddhas in Abhayadatta's list; a ḍākinī[^dakini] told him to eat fish guts to purify his pride. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[lu hi pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/lu_hi_pa)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.vv. "[Luipa](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Luipa)," "[Eighty-four mahasiddhas](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Eighty-four_mahasiddhas)."

[^hundred]: The Perfection of Wisdom in a Hundred Thousand Lines is the longest of the Perfection of Wisdom sutras. Once the manuscript calls it the Eight Thousand, a shorter one, and four times the Hundred Thousand; the English keeps to the Hundred Thousand. See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud*, fols. 4a, 4b; *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[shes rab kyi pha rol tu phyin pa stong phrag brgya pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/shes_rab_kyi_pha_rol_tu_phyin_pa_stong_phrag_brgya_pa)."

[^two-stages]: The two stages (*rim gnyis*) are the stage of generation[^generation], in which one imagines oneself as the deity, and the stage of completion[^completion], in which one dissolves it and works with the subtle body; together they are the meditation of the tantras[^tantra]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[rim gnyis](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rim_gnyis)," "[bskyed rdzogs](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bskyed_rdzogs)."

[^sugata]: The Sugatas, "the Blissfully Gone" (Tibetan *bde bar gshegs pa*), are the buddhas, gone to bliss by a blissful path. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[bde bar gshegs pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bde_bar_gshegs_pa)."

[^conduct]: Conduct (Sanskrit *caryā*, Tibetan *spyod pa*) is how a practitioner acts; in the tantras[^tantra], the unconventional conduct by which a yogin[^yogin] who has gained realization brings it to its end. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[spyod pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/spyod_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 808.

[^accomplishment]: Accomplishment is siddhi[^siddhi]; "the accomplishment that is close to the supreme Mahāmudrā[^mahamudra]" is close to complete awakening. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dngos grub](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dngos_grub)."

[^delighted]: That Tilopa and Bharima "delighted each other in the conduct[^conduct] of the secret mantra[^secret-mantra]" means they practiced together as consorts, karmamudrā, the "action seal": a practice with a physical consort, joined with inner heat[^inner-heat], that turns passion into great bliss[^great-bliss] and insight into emptiness[^emptiness]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[las kyi phyag rgya](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/las_kyi_phyag_rgya)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Karmamudrā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karmamudr%C4%81)."

[^secret-mantra]: The secret mantra (Sanskrit *guhyamantra*, Tibetan *gsang sngags*) is another name for the Vajrayāna, the path of the tantras[^tantra], secret because it is given only to those who have been empowered. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[gsang sngags](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gsang_sngags)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Secret mantra](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Secret_mantra)."

[^bone-ornaments]: The bone ornaments (*rus rgyan*) are the six ornaments of carved human bone worn by wrathful deities and yogins[^yogin]: for a male deity a crown of bone pendants, earrings, a short and a long necklace, a girdle, and bracelets and anklets. They stand for the six perfections. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rus pa'i rgyan drug](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rus_pa'i_rgyan_drug)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Bone ornaments](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Bone_ornaments)."

[^innate]: The innate (Sanskrit *sahaja*, Tibetan *lhan cig skyes pa*, "born together") is the awakened nature that arises together with the mind itself[^mind-itself] and was never apart from it. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[lhan cig skyes pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/lhan_cig_skyes_pa)."

[^freed]: To be freed (*grol ba*) is liberation (*thar pa*, *vimokṣa*): release from saṃsāra[^samsara], or here the moment of realizing the nature of mind[^nature-of-mind]; throughout the book Tilopa's words free those who hear them. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[grol ba](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/grol_ba)," "[thar pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/thar_pa)."

[^vajra-song]: A vajra song (*rdo rje'i mgur*) is a song of realization sung by a master; *mgur* is the song a guru[^guru] sings of realization or the Dharma[^dharma], as Milarepa's are. A vajra (*rdo rje*) is Indra's thunderbolt and the diamond, in the tantras[^tantra] the sign of what cannot be broken; "vajra" before a word makes it of that kind. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rdo rje'i mgur](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rdo_rje'i_mgur)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 282, 703.

[^brahma]: Brahmā (Tibetan *tshangs pa*) is the ruler of the gods[^god] of the form realm; a voice like Brahmā's is deep and moving, and the Brahmā voice is a mark of a buddha's speech. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[tshangs pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/tshangs_pa)," "[tshangs dbyangs](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/tshangs_dbyangs)."

[^dependent-arising]: Dependent arising (Sanskrit *pratītyasamutpāda*, Tibetan *rten cing 'brel bar 'byung ba*) is the teaching that things arise neither without a cause nor from a creator, but only where causes and conditions meet; its "links" are the twelve, from ignorance[^ignorance] to old age and death. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rten cing 'brel bar 'byung ba](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rten_cing_'brel_bar_'byung_ba)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Twelve links of dependent origination](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Twelve_links_of_dependent_origination)."

[^primordial-wisdom]: Primordial wisdom (Sanskrit *jñāna*, Tibetan *ye shes*) is the timeless awareness[^awareness] of the awakened mind, present from the beginning. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[ye shes](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/ye_shes)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 1142.

[^suchness]: Suchness (Sanskrit *tathatā*, Tibetan *de bzhin nyid*) is things just as they are, another name for emptiness[^emptiness] and the nature of things[^nature-of-things]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[de bzhin nyid](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/de_bzhin_nyid)," "[de nyid](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/de_nyid)."

[^signs]: Signs (*brda*) are the gestures, symbols and words by which a master shows what words cannot say; so in 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī[^wisdom-dakini] answers Tilopa in signs. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[brda](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/brda)."

[^kye-ho]: Kye ho! is a cry of amazement that calls the hearer, "Behold!", often opening a song of realization. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[kye ho](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/kye_ho)."

[^remedy]: A remedy (Sanskrit *pratipakṣa*, Tibetan *gnyen po*) is a practice that counters a fault; where all is the innate[^innate], the song says, there is none to apply. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[gnyen po](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gnyen_po)."

[^grounds-paths]: The grounds and paths (*sa lam*) are the ten grounds of the bodhisattva and the five paths, the stages by which most teachings measure the way to buddhahood[^buddhahood]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[sa lam](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sa_lam)."

[^convention]: Convention (Sanskrit *saṃvṛti*, Tibetan *kun rdzob*, "concealing") is the relative level of truth, where words and signs[^signs] work, as against the ultimate[^ultimate], which they cannot reach. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[kun rdzob](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/kun_rdzob)."

[^four-transmissions]: The four transmissions (*bka' babs bzhi*) are the four lines of teaching Tilopa received from his four human gurus[^guru]: illusory body[^illusory-body] with transference, dream[^dream], luminosity[^luminosity] and inner heat[^inner-heat]. The name Kagyü is sometimes said to mean "the lineage[^lineage] of the four transmissions". See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[bka' babs bzhi](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bka'_babs_bzhi)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Kagyü](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Kagy%C3%BC)."

[^illusory-body]: The illusory body (*sgyu lus*) is the practice of knowing one's body and all things as an illusion, one of the six doctrines of Nāropa. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[sgyu lus](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sgyu_lus)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 329.

[^inner-heat]: Inner heat (Sanskrit *caṇḍālī*, Tibetan *gtum mo*) is the yoga of kindling heat through the channels and winds[^channels-winds] until the vital winds enter the central channel and great bliss[^great-bliss] arises; it is the root of the six doctrines of Nāropa. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[gtum mo](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gtum_mo)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Tummo](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Tummo)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Tummo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tummo)."

[^pledge]: A pledge (Sanskrit *samaya*, Tibetan *dam tshig*) is a commitment of the Vajrayāna taken at empowerment[^empowerment]: outwardly to keep harmony with the guru[^guru] and one's companions, inwardly never to stray from the practice. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dam tshig](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dam_tshig)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Samaya](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Samaya)."

[^fortune]: Fortune (*skal ba*) is one's karmic lot, the merit that makes one fit for a teaching; one "with the fortune" is a worthy recipient. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[skal ba](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/skal_ba)," "[skal ldan](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/skal_ldan)."

[^ladder]: The text does not say what the "pair of grass mice" are, a key or a charm; they open the gate. See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud*, fols. 5b, 6a.

[^reference]: Reference (*dmigs pa*) is the mind's fixing on an object as this or that, a frame of reference; "where reference has ceased" the mind grasps at nothing. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dmigs pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dmigs_pa)."

[^non-duality]: Non-duality (Sanskrit *advaya*, Tibetan *gnyis su med pa*) is the absence of two, of subject and object, saṃsāra[^samsara] and nirvāṇa, bliss and emptiness[^emptiness]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[gnyis su med pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gnyis_su_med_pa)," "[gnyis med](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gnyis_med)."

[^five-families]: The five families (*rigs lnga*) are the five buddha families, buddha, vajra, jewel, lotus and action, each with its buddha (Vairocana, Akṣobhya, Ratnasambhava, Amitābha, Amoghasiddhi), its wisdom and the poison of the mind it purifies. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rigs lnga](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rigs_lnga)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 1180; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Five buddha families](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Five_buddha_families)."

[^dakini-of-action]: The ḍākinī of action (Sanskrit *karmaḍākinī*, Tibetan *las kyi mkha' 'gro*) is a ḍākinī[^dakini] who carries out enlightened activity, as against the wisdom ḍākinī[^wisdom-dakini]; the Nyingma speak of ḍākinīs of action, of wisdom and of the world. Here she keeps the gate. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[las kyi mkha' 'gro](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/las_kyi_mkha'_'gro)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Dakini](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Dakini)."

[^devotion]: Devotion (*mos gus*), longing and respect toward the guru[^guru], is what the Kagyü, "the Practice Lineage", stress with guru yoga and the transmission from master to disciple. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[mos gus](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/mos_gus)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Kagyü](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Kagy%C3%BC)."

[^discipline-of-awareness]: The discipline of awareness (*rig pa brtul zhugs*) is the yogin's discipline[^discipline] carried by awareness[^awareness]: Tilopa overwhelms the ḍākinīs[^dakini] by resting unmoved in body, speech and mind. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rig pa brtul zhugs](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rig_pa_brtul_zhugs)."

[^kye-ma]: Kye ma! is a cry of surprise and sorrow, "Alas!" See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[kye ma](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/kye_ma)."

[^heroes]: Heroes and heroines (*dpa' bo*, *dpa' mo*; Sanskrit *vīra*, *vīriṇī*) are the male and female deities of a maṇḍala[^mandala], the ḍākas and ḍākinīs[^dakini]; they ring the wisdom ḍākinī[^wisdom-dakini] as Cakrasaṃvara's[^cakrasamvara] retinue rings him. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dpa' mo](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dpa'_mo)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 788.

[^blessed-lady]: The Blessed Lady (Sanskrit *bhagavatī*, Tibetan *bcom ldan 'das ma*) is the female of the title Blessed One[^blessed-one]. As "mother of all the buddhas" the wisdom ḍākinī[^wisdom-dakini] is the Perfection of Wisdom, *prajñāpāramitā*, called the mother of the victorious ones. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[bcom ldan 'das ma](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bcom_ldan_'das_ma)," "[rgyal ba'i yum](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rgyal_ba'i_yum)."

[^view]: The view (*lta ba*) is one's understanding of how things are; with conduct[^conduct], the fruit[^fruit] and the pledge[^pledge] it makes the whole of a practitioner's way. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[lta ba](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/lta_ba)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 541.

[^painted-card]: A painted card (*tsa ka li*) is a small painting of a deity, a maṇḍala[^mandala] or an offering, used in rites and empowerments[^empowerment]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[tsa ka li](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/tsa_ka_li)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Tsakli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsakli)."

[^seed-syllable]: A seed syllable (*yig 'bru*, *sa bon*) is the syllable from which a deity arises in the stage of generation[^generation]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[yig 'bru](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/yig_'bru)."

[^hand-emblem]: A hand emblem (*phyag mtshan*) is the object a deity holds, which identifies it and stands for its qualities. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[phyag mtshan](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/phyag_mtshan)."

[^emptiness]: Emptiness (Sanskrit *śūnyatā*, Tibetan *stong pa nyid*) is the lack of any fixed, independent being in anything, the illusory nature of all things; "appearance and emptiness" are things appearing while empty, never apart. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[stong pa nyid](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/stong_pa_nyid)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 552.

[^free-of-thought]: Thought (*rnam rtog*) is conceptual thinking; to be free of thought (*rnam par mi rtog pa*) is the state of recognizing reality freed[^freed] of its distortions. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[rnam rtog](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rnam_rtog)," "[rnam par mi rtog pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rnam_par_mi_rtog_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 759.

[^way-things-are]: The way things are (*gnas lugs*) is the abiding nature of reality, things as they are. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[gnas lugs](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gnas_lugs)."

[^ignorance]: Ignorance (Sanskrit *avidyā*, Tibetan *ma rig pa*) is not recognizing the nature of mind[^nature-of-mind] and of things; it is the first of the twelve links of dependent arising[^dependent-arising] and the root of saṃsāra[^samsara]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[ma rig pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/ma_rig_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 950; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Twelve links of dependent origination](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Twelve_links_of_dependent_origination)."

[^self-aware]: Self-aware (*rang rig*), self-arisen (*rang byung*) and self-illumined (*rang gsal*) describe wisdom: it knows itself, occurs of itself, and is clear by its own nature. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[rang rig](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rang_rig)," "[rang byung](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rang_byung)," "[rang gsal](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rang_gsal)."

[^mind-itself]: Mind itself (*sems nyid*) is the nature of mind[^nature-of-mind], a name for buddha nature, as against the thoughts that pass through it. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[sems nyid](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sems_nyid)."

[^self-freed]: Self-freed (*rang grol*): a thought or feeling freed[^freed] by itself, unraveling of its own accord with no remedy[^remedy] applied. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rang grol](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rang_grol)."

[^nature-of-things]: The nature of things (Sanskrit *dharmatā*, Tibetan *chos nyid*) is the ultimate[^ultimate] nature of all that is, emptiness[^emptiness], which the song makes one with mind itself[^mind-itself] and the dharma body[^dharma-body]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[chos nyid](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/chos_nyid)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 431.

[^blessed-one]: The Blessed One (Sanskrit *bhagavān*, Tibetan *bcom ldan 'das*, "the victorious one who has gone beyond") is a title of a buddha. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[bcom ldan 'das](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bcom_ldan_'das)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 395.

[^root-tantra]: A root tantra (Sanskrit *mūlatantra*, Tibetan *rtsa rgyud*) is the chief scripture of a cycle, here the root tantra of Cakrasaṃvara[^cakrasamvara] in fifty-one chapters; an explanatory tantra[^tantra] (*bshad rgyud*) explains it. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[rtsa rgyud](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rtsa_rgyud)," "[bshad rgyud](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bshad_rgyud)."

[^generation]: The stage of generation (Sanskrit *utpattikrama*, Tibetan *bskyed rim*), the first of the two stages[^two-stages], is the whole meditation from dissolving into emptiness[^emptiness] to the full image of the maṇḍala[^mandala] and its deities. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[bskyed rim](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bskyed_rim)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Kyerim](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Kyerim)."

[^essence]: "The essence" (*snying po*) is most likely the heart mantra, the essential mantra of a deity, which is recited to accomplish its speech. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[snying po](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/snying_po)."

[^completion]: The stage of completion (Sanskrit *utpannakrama*, Tibetan *rdzogs rim*), the second of the two stages[^two-stages], is the dissolving of the visualization and the yogas of the channels, winds and essences of the subtle body. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rdzogs rim](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rdzogs_rim)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Dzogrim](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Dzogrim)."

[^worthy-vessels]: Worthy vessels (*snod ldan*) are disciples open and fit to receive the teaching. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[snod ldan](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/snod_ldan)."

[^nine-cycles]: The nine cycles of the bodiless ḍākinīs[^dakini] (*lus med mkha' 'gro'i chos skor dgu*) are nine teachings of the whispered lineage[^whispered-lineage], given in the nine lines that follow, which Tilopa receives from ḍākinīs without bodies, out of the sky. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[lus med mkha' 'gro'i chos skor dgu](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/lus_med_mkha'_'gro'i_chos_skor_dgu)."

[^channels-winds]: The channels and winds (Sanskrit *nāḍī* and *prāṇa*, Tibetan *rtsa rlung*) are the subtle body of the tantras[^tantra], its channels and the energies that move in them, and the yogas that work with them in the stage of completion[^completion]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rtsa rlung](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rtsa_rlung)."

[^awareness]: Awareness (*rig pa*) is the knowing, awake quality of mind, mind's nature recognized; its absence is ignorance[^ignorance]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rig pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rig_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 1178.

[^sacred-substance]: A sacred substance (*dam rdzas*) is a substance blessed by mantra and taken as a pledge[^pledge], a sacrament. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dam rdzas](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dam_rdzas)."

[^one-taste]: One taste (*ro snyoms*, *ro gcig*) is the realization that everything has the same taste, the nature of mind[^nature-of-mind]; in Mahāmudrā[^mahamudra] it is the third of the four yogas. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[ro snyoms](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/ro_snyoms)," "[ro gcig](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/ro_gcig)."

[^all-knowing]: The All-Knowing (*thams cad mkhyen pa*) is an epithet of a buddha. The Kagyü say Tilopa received teachings directly from the primordial buddha Vajradhara, of whom he is also called an emanation[^emanation]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[thams cad mkhyen pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/thams_cad_mkhyen_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 572; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Kagyü](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Kagy%C3%BC)."

[^heretic]: A heretic (Sanskrit *tīrthika*, Tibetan *mu stegs pa*) is a teacher or follower of a non-Buddhist school of India, Hindu, Jain or materialist. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[mu stegs pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/mu_stegs_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 967.

[^acts-no-fruit]: Acts (Sanskrit *karma*, Tibetan *las*) bear fruit[^fruit], in the Buddhist teaching: every act brings its result to the one who did it, the karmic law of cause and effect (*las 'bras*). One who says acts bear no fruit denies karma, and with it any reason for virtue[^virtue]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[las 'bras](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/las_'bras)."

[^order]: The other tellings of Tilopa's life tame the same people in another order. The lives of the gurus[^guru] of the whispered lineage[^whispered-lineage] in a later manuscript make the heretic[^heretic] the fifth and the beer-seller the seventh, and their beer-seller is tamed when Tilopa holds the sun in the sky until her credit falls due; Tsangnyön's *Gzhung 'brel*, in both manuscripts of the 1971 Leh reprint, makes the beer-seller follow the magician and has Tilopa come to her as a beggar; the life in Marpa's collected works tames the magician, the beer-seller, a hunter, a heretic teacher, a sorcerer, the singer and a strong man. See Shar kha Ras chen et al., *Brgyud pa yid bzhin nor bu'i rnam thar dang zhal gdams sogs*; *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud (Ras chung snyan rgyud): Two Manuscript Collections*; Mar pa Chos kyi blo gros, "Tai lo shes rab bzang po'i rnam thar," in *Mar pa chos kyi blo gros kyi gsung 'bum*.

[^siddha]: The siddhas are the accomplished ones[^accomplished-ones], the yogins[^yogin] of the tantras[^tantra] who have won siddhi[^siddhi]; the paṇḍitas[^pandita] are the scholars. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[grub thob](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/grub_thob)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Mahasiddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahasiddha)."

[^rite-of-the-ground]: The rite of the ground (*sa chog*) takes hold of the site where a maṇḍala[^mandala] will be made. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[sa chog](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sa_chog)."

[^kusali]: A kusāli (Tibetan also *ku su lu*) is a yogin[^yogin] of the simplest life, a beggar who does what comes naturally, as against the learned paṇḍitas[^pandita]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[ku su lu](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/ku_su_lu)."

[^e-ma]: E ma! is a cry of wonder, "How wonderful!", and sometimes of sorrow. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[e ma](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/e_ma)."

[^ultimate]: The ultimate (Sanskrit *paramārtha*, Tibetan *don dam*) is ultimate truth, as against the conventional truth of everyday appearance. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[don dam](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/don_dam)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 644.

[^beyond-thought]: Beyond thought (*bsam gyis mi khyab pa*), inconceivable, unfathomable by ordinary thought: each taming closes with Tilopa speaking of the art of the one he tames as beyond thought, and all who hear are freed[^freed]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[bsam gyis mi khyab pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bsam_gyis_mi_khyab_pa)."

[^without-passing-away]: "Without passing away" (*'das grongs med par*): without dying; each of those Tilopa tames lives on in a sacred place. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "['das grongs](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'das_grongs)."

[^nalanda]: Nālandā, in today's Bihar, was the largest and most famous of the monastic universities of India, founded in the fifth century and still teaching, though largely in ruins, when a Tibetan pilgrim studied there in 1235; "Śrī", like "glorious", is a title of honor. See *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Nalanda University](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Nalanda_University)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Nalanda mahavihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalanda_mahavihara)."

[^outsiders-insiders]: Outsiders and insiders (*phyi rol pa*, *nang pa*): the non-Buddhists and the Buddhists. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[phyi rol pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/phyi_rol_pa)," "[nang pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/nang_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 734.

[^three-realms]: The three realms (Sanskrit *tridhātu*, Tibetan *khams gsum*) are the realm of desire, the realm of form and the formless realm, the whole of saṃsāra[^samsara]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[khams gsum](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/khams_gsum)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 142; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Three realms](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Three_realms)."

[^vase]: To fill the vase with wind is vase breathing (*bum pa can*, Sanskrit *kumbhaka*), the holding of the breath in yoga. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[bum pa can](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bum_pa_can)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Kumbhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumbhaka)."

[^cool-grove]: The Cool Grove (Sanskrit *Śītavana*, Tibetan *bsil ba'i tshal*) is the first of the eight great charnel grounds[^charnel-ground], near Bodh Gaya. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[bsil ba'i tshal](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bsil_ba'i_tshal)," "[dur khrod brgyad](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dur_khrod_brgyad)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Shitavana](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Shitavana)."

[^sin]: Sin (*sdig pa*) is a harmful act, a misdeed, which brings suffering to the one who does it; the opposite of virtue[^virtue]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[sdig pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sdig_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 714.

[^man-of-illusion]: The man of illusion (*sgyu ma'i skyes bu*) is a phantom, a person in a magical illusion, a stock image of the teachings for beings that seem to live and are empty. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[sgyu ma'i skyes bu](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sgyu_ma'i_skyes_bu)," "[sgyu ma](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sgyu_ma)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 329.

[^attahasa]: Aṭṭahāsa, "Loud Laughter" (Tibetan *ha ha rgod pa*), is one of the eight great charnel grounds[^charnel-ground]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[ha ha rgod pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/ha_ha_rgod_pa)."

[^magician]: The magician's question and the first lines of Tilopa's answer stand also in Tsangnyön's *Gzhung 'brel*, in its Jatang manuscript: "sgyu ma'i skyes bu bsod … sems med phyir na sdig pa med", "the man of illusion[^man-of-illusion] … since he has no mind, there is no sin[^sin]". See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud (Ras chung snyan rgyud): Two Manuscript Collections*, 1:34.

[^ifyou]: The life in Marpa's collected works gathers lines from the songs of these tamings into one long song Tilopa sings to Nāropa: "if you thirst, drink the water of poison; / if you grieve, look at the city of the gandharvas[^gandharva]; / if you sink, fill the vase[^vase] with wind; / if you stray, chase the monkey and kill it; … / if you cling, go into the charnel ground[^charnel-ground]". See Mar pa Chos kyi blo gros, "Tai lo shes rab bzang po'i rnam thar," in *Mar pa chos kyi blo gros kyi gsung 'bum*.

[^singer]: The life in Marpa's collected works names the singer of Nagara "the Mute with the Spear", *mdung can lkug pa*. See Mar pa Chos kyi blo gros, "Tai lo shes rab bzang po'i rnam thar," in *Mar pa chos kyi blo gros kyi gsung 'bum*.

[^fire-end-of-time]: The fire at the end of time (*dus mtha'i me*) is the fire at the end of the aeon that burns up the universe. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dus mtha'i me](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dus_mtha'i_me)."

[^wind-mind]: The wind-mind (*rlung sems*) is the mind and the energies it rides, which are closely joined. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rlung sems](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rlung_sems)."

[^crown]: To draw the wind-mind[^wind-mind] out through the crown is transference (*'pho ba*), the ejection of consciousness to a buddha field at death, one of the six doctrines of Nāropa. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "['pho ba](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'pho_ba)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Phowa](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Phowa)."

[^raksasa]: Rākṣasas (*srin po*) are cannibal demons[^demon], one of the eight classes of gods[^god] and demons; their land is Laṅkā. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[srin po](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/srin_po)," "[lang ka](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/lang_ka)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 1290; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Rakshasa](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Rakshasa)."

[^butcher]: The life in Marpa's collected works tells the butcher's story the same way: Tilopa hides the son and makes the meat in the copper pot appear as the son's flesh, and the butcher gives up killing. See Mar pa Chos kyi blo gros, "Tai lo shes rab bzang po'i rnam thar," in *Mar pa chos kyi blo gros kyi gsung 'bum*.

[^carvaka]: A Cārvāka (Tibetan *rgyang phan pa*), or Lokāyata, is a follower of the materialist school of ancient India, which trusted only perception. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rgyang phan pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rgyang_phan_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 308; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Charvaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charvaka)."

[^virtue]: Virtue (*dge ba*) is a wholesome act, which brings happiness to the one who does it; the opposite of sin[^sin]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dge ba](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dge_ba)."

[^hell]: The hells (Sanskrit *naraka*, Tibetan *dmyal ba*), eight hot and eight cold, are the lowest realms of saṃsāra[^samsara]; a being is born there by its own karma, not by judgment, and stays only until that karma is spent. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dmyal ba](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dmyal_ba)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 983; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Naraka (Buddhism)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naraka_%28Buddhism%29)."

[^higher-realms]: The higher realms (*mtho ris*) are rebirth as a god[^god] or a human. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[mtho ris](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/mtho_ris)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 603.

[^victory-banner]: The victory banner (*rgyal mtshan*) is one of the eight auspicious symbols, the banner of the victory of Buddhism. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rgyal mtshan](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rgyal_mtshan)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 313; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Eight auspicious symbols](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Eight_auspicious_symbols)."

[^discursive-thought]: Discursive thought (*rnam rtog*) is conceptual thinking; that it "is the emanation body[^emanation-body]" says thought itself appears, as an emanation body does, from the nature of mind[^nature-of-mind]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rnam rtog](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rnam_rtog)."

[^sriparvata]: Śrīparvata (Tibetan *dpal gyi ri*), a mountain in the south of India, is where Nāgārjuna[^nagarjuna] spent his last days. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dpal gyi ri](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dpal_gyi_ri)."

[^sorcerer]: The Tibetan of this passage is hard, and the English gives its likeliest sense: the two are matched in killing; Tilopa takes up all who died but some, the sorcerer's women among them, and offers to raise those too if the sorcerer gives up his magic. See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud*, fols. 10a, 10b.

[^samsara]: Saṃsāra (Tibetan *'khor ba*, "circling") is the round of birth and death among the six classes of beings. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "['khor ba](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'khor_ba)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 190.

[^abbot-master]: The abbot and master (*mkhan slob*) are the preceptor and instructor who give a monk his ordination; here Tilopa's own uncle and mother. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[mkhan slob](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/mkhan_slob)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 179.

[^three-wheels]: The three wheels (*'khor lo gsum*) of a monk's work are study, meditation and work. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "['khor lo gsum](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'khor_lo_gsum)."

[^provost]: The provost (*dge skos*) is the monk who keeps discipline in a monastery. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[dge skos](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dge_skos)," "[dge bskos](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dge_bskos)."

[^birds]: The word for what he killed, *cha ga ba*, is not in the dictionaries; that they fly away at the end of the scene makes them small birds. See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud*, fol. 10b.

[^kalpa]: A kalpa (Tibetan *bskal pa*) is an aeon, the vast span in which a universe forms, lasts and passes. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[bskal pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bskal_pa)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 121; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Kalpa](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Kalpa)."

[^buddha-fields]: A buddha field (*zhing khams*) is the pure realm of a buddha, where buddhas teach in the enjoyment body[^enjoyment-body]. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[zhing khams](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/zhing_khams)."

[^aryadeva]: Āryadeva (Tibetan *'phags pa lha*) was Nāgārjuna's[^nagarjuna] disciple and continued his Middle Way; he too is counted among the eighty-four mahāsiddhas. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "['phags pa lha](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'phags_pa_lha)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Aryadeva](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Aryadeva)."

[^thousand-buddhas]: The thousand buddhas are the thousand who appear in this "good aeon" (Sanskrit *bhadrakalpa*, Tibetan *bskal pa bzang po*). See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[bskal pa bzang po](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bskal_pa_bzang_po)."

[^yoke]: A yoke's length (*gnya' shing*) ahead is as far as Tilopa's eyes look as he walks for alms. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[gnya' shing](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gnya'_shing)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 491.

[^giving-receiving]: The blessings[^blessing] of giving and receiving (*sbyin len gyi shis pa*) are words of good fortune[^fortune] spoken over alms given and received. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[sbyin len](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/sbyin_len)," "[shis pa](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/shis_pa)."

[^three-times]: The three times (*dus gsum*) are past, present and future. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[dus gsum](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dus_gsum)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 635.

[^colored-powders]: A maṇḍala[^mandala] of colored powders (*rdul tshon*) is drawn on the ground in powdered stone for a rite. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rdul tshon](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rdul_tshon)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Mandala](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Mandala)."

[^mahamaya]: Mahāmāyā, "Great Illusion" (Tibetan *sgyu ma chen mo*), is one of the mother tantras[^tantra] of the highest yoga tantra. See *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Mahamaya](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Mahamaya)."

[^aggregates-elements]: The aggregates (Sanskrit *skandha*, Tibetan *phung po*) are the five heaps that make a person: form, feeling, perception, formations and consciousness; the elements (*khams*) are earth, water, fire, air and space. Each aggregate is purified as one of the five buddha families. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[phung khams](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/phung_khams)," "['byung ba lnga](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/'byung_ba_lnga)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 824; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Five buddha families](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Five_buddha_families)."

[^vajrabhairava]: Vajrabhairava, "Vajra Terror" (Tibetan *rdo rje 'jigs byed*), a form of Yamāntaka, "Destroyer of Death", has a buffalo's face and many arms and legs; his tantra[^tantra] belongs to the father tantras. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[rdo rje 'jigs byed](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/rdo_rje_'jigs_byed)"; *Rigpa Wiki*, s.v. "[Vajrabhairava](https://www.rigpawiki.org/index.php?title=Vajrabhairava)"; *Wikipedia*, s.v. "[Yamantaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamantaka)."

[^mangalam]: Maṅgalam, Sanskrit for "auspiciousness" (Tibetan *bkra shis*), closes the book as a benediction. See *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.v. "[bkra shis](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/bkra_shis)"; Das, *Tibetan-English Dictionary*, 69.

[^colophon]: Marpa writes at Drowolung, his seat in the Trowo valley of Lhodrak, for his son Dode, Darma Dode. See *Bde mchog mkha' 'gro snyan rgyud*, fol. 11b; *Rangjung Yeshe Wiki*, s.vv. "[gro bo lung](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/gro_bo_lung)," "[dar ma mdo sde](https://rywiki.tsadra.org/index.php/dar_ma_mdo_sde)."
