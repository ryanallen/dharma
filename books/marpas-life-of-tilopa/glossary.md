*The names and words of [The Life of Tilopa](marpas-life-of-tilopa.md), each with its Tibetan, in Wylie, and its Sanskrit where it has one.*

### Aḍona

ཨ་ཌོ་ན *a ḍo na*.

The temple of the monastery of the Crown Jewel.

### Āryadeva

ཨཱ་རྱ་དེ་ཝ *ā rya de wa*; Sanskrit Āryadeva.

Nāgārjuna's disciple, with whom Tilopa says he has talked.

### Aṭṭahāsa

ཧ་ཧ་སྒྲོག་པ *ha ha sgrog pa*; Sanskrit Aṭṭahāsa.

A charnel ground, "Loud Laughter".

### Bengal

བྷང་ག་ལ *bhang ga la*.

The land in the east of India where the prophecy sends Tilopa to serve Bharima, in the market of Pañcapana.

### Bharima

བྷ་རི་མ *bha ri ma*.

The harlot of the market of Pañcapana in Bengal. Foretold, Tilopa serves her household, by night bringing men in and seeing them out and by day pounding sesame; with her he practices the secret mantra in the charnel ground of Kereli, and the two sing the song of the sesame oil before the king.

### Black Virtue

ནག་པོ་དགེ་བ *nag po dge ba*.

The name given the heretic of 1.4.2.

### Bliss-Making Joy

བདེ་བྱེད་དགའ་བ *bde byed dga' ba*.

The name given the butcher of 1.4.6.

### Cakrasaṃvara

བདེ་མཆོག *bde mchog*; Sanskrit Cakrasaṃvara.

The tantric deity of bliss whose emanation, and whose self, Tilopa is shown to be; "Saṃvara" where a song shortens the name. The text also writes *bde mchog 'khor lo*, *'khor lo bde mchog*, *'khor lo sdom pa*.

### Cārvāka

རྒྱང་ཕན་པ *rgyang phan pa*; Sanskrit Cārvāka, Lokāyata.

A materialist, who denies that acts bear fruit.

### Caryāpa

ཙརྱ་པ *tsarya pa*; Sanskrit Caryāpa.

One of the four human gurus, who gave him dream. The manuscript also writes *tsarya ba*.

### the Cool Grove

སིལ་བའི་ཚལ *sil ba'i tshal*; Sanskrit Śītavana.

A charnel ground, Śītavana.

### ḍākinī

མཁའ་འགྲོ་མ *mkha' 'gro ma*; Sanskrit ḍākinī.

A female being of wisdom who guards and gives the secret teachings.

### Deceiver Who Tells the Truth

སླུ་བྱེད་བདེན་སྨྲ *slu byed bden smra*.

The name given the magician of 1.4.3.

### Dode

མདོ་སྡེ *mdo sde*.

Marpa's son, Darma Dode, for whom the life was written.

### Drowolung

གྲོ་བོ་ལུང *gro bo lung*.

Marpa's seat in Lhodrak, where he wrote this life.

### Dukata

དུ་ཀ་ཏ *du ka ta*.

A river bank in the east of India, near the Sorrowless monastery.

### Dzako

ཛ་ཀོ *dza ko*.

Tilopa's birthplace, a city of Zahor in the east of India.

### gandharva

དྲི་ཟ *dri za*; Sanskrit gandharva.

A celestial musician, known for sweet song; the city of the gandharvas is a mirage.

### Gandhola

གྷནདོ་ལ *ghando la*; Sanskrit Gandhola.

The temple in Oḍḍiyāna where the ḍākinīs keep the jewels. The manuscript also writes *ghando li*, *ghando lir*, *ghan do la*.

### Giver of Bliss

བདེ་སྟེར་མ *bde ster ma*.

The ḍākinī the ḍākinīs name as the boy's true sister.

### Guhyasamāja

གསང་བ་འདུས་པ *gsang ba 'dus pa*; Sanskrit Guhyasamāja.

A tantric deity and its tantra.

### Hevajra

ཧེ་བཛྲ *he badzra*; Sanskrit Hevajra.

A tantric deity and its tantra. The song of part 4 names him *kye'i rdo rje*.

### the island of Sosa

སོ་ས་གླིང *so sa gling*.

Where the beer-seller lives on.

### the island of the rākṣasas

སྲིན་པོའི་གླིང *srin po'i gling*; Sanskrit Laṅkā, the island of the rākṣasas.

Where the butcher lives on.

### Jinabodhi

ཛི་ན་བྱང་ཆུབ *dzi na byang chub*; Sanskrit Jinabodhi.

The name given the Cārvāka of 1.4.7.

### Kalapa

ཀ་ལ་པ *ka la pa*.

Tilopa's name as a monk at the Sorrowless monastery.

### Kalpa Zangmo

སྐལ་བ་བཟང་མོ *skal ba bzang mo*.

The ḍākinī among the four human gurus, who gave him inner heat. Her name means "Fortunate Lady"; it is kept in Tibetan, since the text gives no Sanskrit for it.

### Kasori

ཀ་སོ་རི *ka so ri*.

The third of those three.

### Kereli

ཀེ་རེ་ལི *ke re li*.

The charnel ground where Tilopa and Bharima practice the conduct of the secret mantra to its end.

### Khasu

ཁ་སུ *kha su*.

A river there.

### Kimitsikili

ཀི་མི་ཙི་ཀི་ལི *ki mi tsi ki li*.

Where the sorcerer lives on.

### kusāli

ཀུ་ས་ལི *ku sa li*; Sanskrit kusāli.

A yogin of simple practice, as against the learned paṇḍitas.

### Lawapa

ལ་ཝ་པ *la wa pa*; Sanskrit Lavapa, Kambala.

One of the four human gurus, who gave him luminosity, and who slept twelve years at a crossroads. The manuscript also writes *la ba pa*.

### Lūhipa

ལུ་ཧི་པ *lu hi pa*; Sanskrit Lūipa.

The mahāsiddha whose meaning the ḍākinī teaches Tilopa in the temple.

### Mahāmāyā

མ་ཧཱ་མ་ཡ *ma hā ma ya*; Sanskrit Mahāmāyā.

A tantric deity and its tantra.

### Mahāmudrā

ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོ *phyag rgya chen po*; Sanskrit mahāmudrā.

The "great seal", the nature of mind and the practice that realizes it.

### Maheśvara

དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ *dbang phyug chen po*; Sanskrit Maheśvara.

The great god, Śiva, who blessed the charnel ground of Sala Bhehara.

### the Masha Grove

རྨ་ཤའི་ཚལ *rma sha'i tshal*.

The charnel ground of the Sorrowless monastery.

### Melodious Mute

དབྱངས་ལྡན་ལྐུགས་པ *dbyangs ldan lkugs pa*.

The name given the singer of 1.4.5.

### Mighty Intellect

ནུས་ལྡན་བློ་གྲོས *nus ldan blo gros*.

The name given the yogin of 1.4.1.

### the monastery of the Crown Jewel

གཙུག་གི་ནོར་བུའི་དགོན་པ *gtsug gi nor bu'i dgon pa*.

Where the ḍākinī sends Tilopa; its temple is Aḍona.

### nāga

ཀླུ *klu*; Sanskrit nāga.

A serpent spirit of the waters and the underworld, one of the beings the Brahmin who reads the marks thinks the newborn Tilopa may be.

### Nagara

ན་ག་ར *na ga ra*; Sanskrit Nagara.

Where the singer lives on.

### Nāgārjuna

ཀླུ་སྒྲུབ *klu sgrub*; Sanskrit Nāgārjuna.

One of Tilopa's four human gurus, who gave him the illusory body; here the tantric master of that name.

### Nālandā

ནཱ་ལན་དཱ *nā lan dā*; Sanskrit Nālandā.

The great monastery where Tilopa tames the heretic; "glorious Śrī Nālandā", as the manuscript writes it, *dpal shri na len tra*.

### Nāropa

ནཱ་རོ་པ *nā ro pa*; Sanskrit Nāropa, Nāḍapāda.

Tilopa's disciple, the great paṇḍita, who bore twelve hardships under him; Marpa's guru. The homage calls him *nā ro'i paṇ chen*, "the great paṇḍita Nāropa", and the ḍākinī of wisdom *na ro*, "Nāro", with Riri and Kasori.

### Nyiimi

ཉི་ཨི་མི *nyi i mi*.

The name given the sorcerer of 1.4.8, as the Tibetan writes it.

### Oḍḍiyāna

ཨུ་རྒྱན *u rgyan*; Sanskrit Oḍḍiyāna, Uḍḍiyāna.

The land of the ḍākinīs in the west.

### Pañcapana

པཎ་ཙ་པ་ན *paṇ tsa pa na*.

The name the ḍākinīs give the boy in their song, and the name he gives himself at the palace of the ḍākinīs; nearly the name of the market town in Bengal, *paṇ tsha pa na*, where he serves Bharima. The text does not explain it.

### Prajñābhadra

ཤེས་རབ་བཟང་པོ *shes rab bzang po*; Sanskrit Prajñābhadra.

Tilopa's name given by the ḍākinīs in Oḍḍiyāna (1.2), "Excellent Wisdom"; "Tilo Prajñābhadra" for *te lo shes rab bzang po*.

### Rāhu

སྒྲ་གཅན *sgra gcan*; Sanskrit Rāhu.

The titan who swallows the sun and moon in eclipse; "Rāhu the titan" for *lha min sgra gcan*.

### Riri

རི་རི *ri ri*.

One of the three Tilopa is told to take into his care, with Nāro and Kasori.

### Sala Bhehara

ས་ལ་བྷེ་ཧ་ར *sa la bhe ha ra*.

A charnel ground in the south, blessed by Maheśvara.

### Salden

གསལ་ལྡན *gsal ldan*.

Tilopa's mother, a Brahmin. The text also writes *gsal ldan ma*.

### Saldrön

གསལ་སྒྲོན *gsal sgron*.

Tilopa's sister, a Brahmin. The text also writes *gsal ba'i sgron ma*.

### Salwa

གསལ་བ *gsal ba*.

Tilopa's father, a Brahmin of Dzako.

### Salö

གསལ་འོད *gsal 'od*.

Tilopa's name as a boy, "Clear Light", given because the east of India filled with light when he was born.

### siddhi

དངོས་གྲུབ *dngos grub*; Sanskrit siddhi.

An accomplishment, ordinary or supreme.

### Siṃhacandra

སེང་གེ་ཟླ་བ *seng ge zla ba*; Sanskrit Siṃhacandra.

The king who invites the siddhas in part 4.

### Sorrowless

མྱ་ངན་མེད་པ *mya ngan med pa*.

The monastery where Tilopa is ordained.

### Śrīparvata

དཔལ་གྱི་རི *dpal gyi ri*; Sanskrit Śrīparvata.

The mountain where Jinabodhi lives on.

### Sunlight Lamp

ཉི་འོད་སྒྲོན་མ *nyi 'od sgron ma*.

The name given the beer-seller of 1.4.4.

### Tilopa

ཏེ་ལོ་པ *te lo pa*; Sanskrit Tilopa, Tillipa.

The mahāsiddha whose life this is, 988–1069 by the usual reckoning; the first of the Kagyü lineage, Nāropa's guru. The text also writes *ti lo pa*, *te lo*, *te los*, *ti lo pa'i*; "Tilo" where the Tibetan has *te lo* and the line wants it.

### Til Sungzhab

ཏིལ་བསྲུངས་ཞབས *til bsrungs zhabs*.

"The Sesame Keeper", the Tibetan the text gives for the name Tilopa, which he won pounding sesame in the market of Pañcapana.

### the Unrivaled

འགྲན་ཟླ་མེད་པ *'gran zla med pa*.

The proud yogin of 1.4.1, who claims to have no rival.

### Vajrabhairava

རྡོ་རྗེ་འཇིགས་བྱེད *rdo rje 'jigs byed*; Sanskrit Vajrabhairava.

A wrathful tantric deity.

### Vajravārāhī

རྡོ་རྗེ་ཕག་མོ *rdo rje phag mo*; Sanskrit Vajravārāhī.

Cakrasaṃvara's consort, named as the boy's true mother.

### yakṣa

གནོད་སྦྱིན *gnod sbyin*; Sanskrit yakṣa.

A spirit of wealth and of wild places, powerful and quick to harm, one of the beings the Brahmin who reads the marks thinks the newborn Tilopa may be.

### Zahor

ཟ་ཧོར *za hor*; Sanskrit Sahor.

The land in the east of India where Tilopa is born, in the city of Dzako, and whose beings he sets on the path at the end.
