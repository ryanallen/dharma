*The names and words of [The Life of Tilopa](marpas-life-of-tilopa.md), each with its Tibetan, in Wylie, and its Sanskrit where it has one.*

### action seal

ལས་ཀྱི་ཕྱག་རྒྱ *las kyi phyag rgya*; Sanskrit *karmamudrā*.

A practice of the tantras with a physical partner, joined with inner heat, that turns passion into great bliss and the knowing of emptiness. The book's "delighted each other in the conduct of the Secret Mantra" means Tilopa and Bharima practiced it together.

### Aḍona

ཨ་ཌོ་ན *a ḍo na*.

The temple of the monastery of the Crown Jewel. After the ḍākinīs give Tilopa the jewels in Oḍḍiyāna, the wisdom ḍākinī sends Tilopa to the monastery of the Crown Jewel to take Nāro, Riri and Kasori into his care, and Tilopa goes to this temple at the end of 1.2.

### aggregates and elements

ཕུང་པོ *phung po*, ཁམས *khams*; Sanskrit *skandha* and *dhātu*.

The five aggregates that make a person, form, feeling, perception, formations and consciousness, and the elements, earth, water, fire, air and space. Each aggregate is purified as one of the five buddha families, and Tilopa's song in part 4 makes them the Guhyasamāja.

### All-Knowing One

ཐམས་ཅད་མཁྱེན་པ *thams cad mkhyen pa*; Sanskrit *Sarvajña*.

An epithet of a buddha. The Kagyü say Tilopa received the Dharma straight from the primordial buddha Vajradhara, and in 1.3 Tilopa's song says the guru is the All-Knowing One.

### Āryadeva

ཨཱ་རྱ་དེ་ཝ *ā rya de wa*; Sanskrit Āryadeva.

Nāgārjuna's disciple, who carried on Nāgārjuna's Madhyamaka and is counted among the eighty-four mahāsiddhas. In the song Tilopa sings to the ruler at the Sorrowless monastery in part 2, Tilopa says he's talked with Nāgārjuna and Āryadeva.

### asura

ལྷ་མིན *lha min*; Sanskrit asura.

One of the jealous demigods who live inside Mount Meru and wage war on the gods. In Indian myth the asura Rāhu swallows the sun and the moon, which is why they're eclipsed; the opening song makes the whispered lineage the light Rāhu dreads.

### Aṭṭahāsa

ཧ་ཧ་སྒྲོག་པ *ha ha sgrog pa*; Sanskrit Aṭṭahāsa.

One of the eight great charnel grounds; its name means "Loud Laughter". The magician Tilopa tames in 1.4.3, given the name Deceiver Who Tells the Truth, lives on there without dying.

### auspicious verses of giving and receiving

སྦྱིན་ལེན་གྱི་ཤིས་པ *sbyin len gyi shis pa*.

Words of good fortune spoken over gifts of food given and received. In part 3 Tilopa speaks them coming back from begging, and the ruler later asks each of the learned for such a verse.

### awareness

རིག་པ *rig pa*; Sanskrit *vidyā*.

The knowing, awake quality of mind, mind's nature recognized; its absence is ignorance. One of the nine cycles in 1.2 calls it the lamp of wisdom, and Tilopa's yogic discipline of awareness overwhelms the ḍākinīs.

### Bengal

བྷང་ག་ལ *bhang ga la*.

The land in the east of India where the prophecy sends Tilopa to serve Bharima, in the market of Pañcapana. There Tilopa pounds sesame by day, which earns him the name Tilopa.

### beyond elaboration

སྤྲོས་བྲལ *spros bral*; Sanskrit *niṣprapañca*.

Free of everything the mind builds about how things are, such as that they exist or don't exist. It describes the dharmakāya and emptiness, and in Mahāmudrā it's the second of the four yogas. The opening calls the three bodies beyond elaboration and wide as the sky.

### beyond thought

བསམ་གྱིས་མི་ཁྱབ་པ *bsam gyis mi khyab pa*; Sanskrit *acintya*.

Inconceivable, past what ordinary thought can grasp. Each taming in 1.4 closes with Tilopa speaking of the art of the one being tamed as beyond thought, and all who hear are freed.

### Bharima

བྷ་རི་མ *bha ri ma*.

The sex worker of the market of Pañcapana in Bengal. As the prophecy says, Tilopa serves Bharima's household, by night bringing people in and seeing them out and by day pounding sesame; with Bharima, Tilopa practices the Secret Mantra in the charnel ground of Kereli, and the two sing the song of the sesame oil before the ruler.

### Black Virtue

ནག་པོ་དགེ་བ *nag po dge ba*.

The name given the non-Buddhist of 1.4.2. A non-Buddhist teacher and siddha at Nālandā made all who didn't rise before them compete in power and in debate; Tilopa beat them at both, and they live on in Śītavana.

### Blessed Lady

བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས་མ *bcom ldan 'das ma*; Sanskrit *Bhagavatī*.

The title Blessed One borne by a deity in female form. In 1.2 the retinue of the wisdom ḍākinī call the ḍākinī "our Blessed Lady" and are angry that Tilopa doesn't bow.

### Blessed One

བཅོམ་ལྡན་འདས *bcom ldan 'das*; Sanskrit *Bhagavān*.

A title of a buddha, in Tibetan "the victorious one who has gone beyond". In 1.2 the ḍākinīs sing it to Tilopa: "Father we all share, Blessed One, Buddha Tilo".

### blessing

བྱིན་རླབས *byin rlabs*; Sanskrit *adhiṣṭhāna*.

The power of the buddhas, a guru or the ḍākinīs passed into a practitioner, a stream that makes realization ripen; to bless is to give it. In the opening song Marpa asks the reader's pardon and blessing for writing the whispered lineage down for Dode.

### Bliss-Making Joy

བདེ་བྱེད་དགའ་བ *bde byed dga' ba*.

The name given the butcher of 1.4.6. A butcher who had killed the young of every living thing saw their own child's limbs in the meat they cooked; they swore off killing, and they live on the island of the rākṣasas without dying.

### bone ornaments

རུས་རྒྱན *rus rgyan*.

The six ornaments of carved human bone worn by wrathful deities and yogins, among them a crown of bone, earrings, necklaces, a girdle, and bracelets and anklets; they stand for the six perfections. In 1.1 some of the people see fire blazing in Tilopa's.

### Brahmā

ཚངས་པ *tshangs pa*; Sanskrit Brahmā.

The ruler of the gods of the form realm. A voice like Brahmā's is deep and moving, and the Brahmā voice is a mark of a buddha's speech; Tilopa and Bharima sing their vajra song in 1.1 in such a voice.

### Brahmin

བྲམ་ཟེ *bram ze*; Sanskrit *brāhmaṇa*.

A member of the priestly class of Indian society, the highest of the four classes, the keepers of the rites. Tilopa's whole family are Brahmins, so the book often calls Tilopa "the Brahmin's child".

### buddha field

ཞིང་ཁམས *zhing khams*; Sanskrit *buddhakṣetra*.

A buddha's own pure world, where buddhas teach in the saṃbhogakāya and beings of great merit can be born. Tilopa's song in part 2 says Tilopa has walked a hundred of them.

### buddhahood

སངས་རྒྱས *sangs rgyas*.

The state of a buddha, one fully awakened from the sleep of ignorance; the Buddha of history is one buddha among countless others. The opening says a disciple of the lineage seeks it in their own mind, in this one life and this one body.

### Cakrasaṃvara

བདེ་མཆོག *bde mchog*; Sanskrit Cakrasaṃvara.

The tantric deity of bliss whose emanation, and whose self, Tilopa is shown to be; "Saṃvara" where a song shortens the name. The text also writes *bde mchog 'khor lo*, *'khor lo bde mchog*, *'khor lo sdom pa*.

### Cārvāka

རྒྱང་ཕན་པ *rgyang phan pa*; Sanskrit Cārvāka, Lokāyata.

A materialist, who denies that acts bear fruit. In 1.4.7 a Cārvāka says there's neither virtue nor wrongdoing; Tilopa shows them the gods and the hells, they're terrified, and they're given the name Jinabodhi.

### Caryāpa

ཙརྱ་པ *tsarya pa*; Sanskrit Caryāpa.

One of the four human gurus, who gave Tilopa dream. A mahāsiddha of India, "the one of conduct", who the child asks for instruction in the charnel ground of Sala Bhehara in 1.1. The manuscript also writes *tsarya ba*.

### channels-and-winds

རྩ་རླུང *rtsa rlung*; Sanskrit *nāḍī* and *prāṇa*.

The subtle body of the tantras, its channels and the energies that move in them, and the yogas that work with them in the stage of completion. One of the nine cycles in 1.2 tells the practitioner to turn their wheel.

### charnel ground

དུར་ཁྲོད *dur khrod*; Sanskrit *śmaśāna*.

Where the dead were left, burned or cut up for the birds. Terrifying and lonely, it's where the yogins of the tantras practiced, and it stands for the death of clinging. Tilopa meets gurus and tames others in charnel grounds throughout the book.

### colored powders

རྡུལ་ཚོན *rdul tshon*.

Powdered stone in many colors, used to draw a maṇḍala on the ground for a rite. In part 4 Tilopa raises a maṇḍala of colored powders in the sky, and each person present sees it differently.

### compassion

ཐུགས་རྗེ *thugs rje*; Sanskrit *karuṇā*.

The wish that beings be free of suffering, and the ever-responsive energy with which an awakened mind acts for them. The opening names it among the marks of a teacher of the lineage, beside experience and realization.

### conduct

སྤྱོད་པ *spyod pa*; Sanskrit *caryā*.

How a practitioner acts; in the tantras, the unconventional conduct by which a yogin who has gained realization brings it to its end. With view, fruition and samaya it's one of the four things a practitioner holds; Tilopa and Bharima bring it to its end at Kereli.

### convention

ཀུན་རྫོབ *kun rdzob*; Sanskrit *saṃvṛti*.

The relative level of truth, where words and signs work, as against the ultimate, which they can't reach. The meaning of the vajra song of 1.1 says that only as convention, in signs, must the guru show it.

### ḍākinī

མཁའ་འགྲོ་མ *mkha' 'gro ma*; Sanskrit ḍākinī.

A female embodiment of awakened energy, "one who goes in the sky": at the highest a wisdom ḍākinī, a buddha in female form; below that, worldly spirits. The ḍākinīs guard the secret Dharma; in this book they prophesy Tilopa's path, test Tilopa and give Tilopa the whispered lineage.

### Deceiver Who Tells the Truth

སླུ་བྱེད་བདེན་སྨྲ *slu byed bden smra*.

The name given the magician of 1.4.3. A magician was taking the ruler's country with an army of illusion; Tilopa broke the illusion and caught them, and they live on at Aṭṭahāsa.

### definitive meaning

ངེས་དོན *nges don*; Sanskrit *nītārtha*.

The real, final meaning, as against the provisional meaning that leads to it. In definitive meaning, the book says, Tilopa was a buddha and had no human guru, and in 1.3 Tilopa sings, "I've no human guru."

### demon

འདྲེ *'dre*.

A harmful spirit of Tibetan belief, with no Sanskrit of its own. In 1.1 the old ones with the marks who come to the newborn child are feared as demons, as a ḍākinī may be by one who doesn't know them; in 1.2 the retinue takes a ḍākinī for a māra.

### dependent arising

རྟེན་ཅིང་འབྲེལ་བར་འབྱུང་བ *rten cing 'brel bar 'byung ba*; Sanskrit *pratītyasamutpāda*.

The Dharma that things arise neither without a cause nor from a creator, only where causes and conditions meet. Its links are the twelve, from ignorance to old age and death. The vajra song of 1.1 says fools may know there's oil in sesame, but unless they know how links arise, they can't press it out.

### devotion

མོས་གུས *mos gus*.

Longing and respect toward the guru, which the Kagyü, "the Practice Lineage", stress above all. In 1.2 the karma ḍākinī gives siddhis to those with faith and devotion, and in part 3 the people grow devoted to Tilopa.

### Dharma

ཆོས *chos*; Sanskrit dharma.

What the Buddha taught and the truth it shows; "the holy Dharma" is the same. A Dharma, with "a", is one body of it passed down, as the whispered lineage is. It's kept in Sanskrit here, as translators from Tibetan keep it.

### dharmakāya

ཆོས་སྐུ *chos sku*; Sanskrit dharmakāya.

The first of the three bodies: the empty, unconditioned truth of awakened mind, which no concept has ever entered. In 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī of the dharmakāya rules in Oḍḍiyāna, and a song there makes it one with mind itself.

### discursive thought

རྣམ་རྟོག *rnam rtog*; Sanskrit *vikalpa*.

Conceptual thinking, one thought leading to the next. A song in 1.4.7 says discursive thought is the nirmāṇakāya: thought itself appears, as a nirmāṇakāya does, out of the nature of mind.

### Dode

མདོ་སྡེ *mdo sde*.

Marpa's son, Darma Dode, who the life was written for. Marpa wrote it down at Drowolung for Dode, and the opening song says that for Dode's sake Marpa set the whispered lineage down in letters.

### dream

རྨི་ལམ *rmi lam*.

The yoga of dream: knowing that one dreams, and training the mind in the dream. It's one of the six dharmas of Nāropa and of the four transmissions; Tilopa asked Caryāpa for it, and the opening song names it as one of the three jewels.

### Drowolung

གྲོ་བོ་ལུང *gro bo lung*.

Marpa's seat in Lhodrak, where he wrote this life. The colophon says Marpa wrote the life down at the monastery of glorious Drowolung, in the Trowo valley of Lhodrak, for Dode.

### Dukata

དུ་ཀ་ཏ *du ka ta*.

A river bank in the east of India, near the Sorrowless monastery. There, at the source of the river Khasu, in the charnel ground of the Masha Grove, stands the monastery where Tilopa is ordained in part 2.

### Dzako

ཛ་ཀོ *dza ko*.

Tilopa's birthplace, a city of Zahor in the east of India. Tilopa's family, Salwa, Salden and Saldrön, live there, and the child names it as his country when the ḍākinīs ask in 1.1.

### e ma

ཨེ་མ *e ma*.

A cry of wonder, "How wonderful!", and sometimes of grief; the short form of *e ma ho*, "How marvelous!". Unlike *kye ho* it calls no one. All cry it in 1.4.1, amazed at Tilopa's magic.

### emanation

སྤྲུལ་པ *sprul pa*; Sanskrit *nirmita*.

A form an awakened being sends out or takes on by its power, a body, a person or a whole scene. Part 2 of the book tells Tilopa as an emanation of Cakrasaṃvara, and part 1.4 shows the eight emanations by which Tilopa tamed others.

### empowerment

དབང་བསྐུར *dbang bskur*; Sanskrit *abhiṣeka*.

The rite of the Vajrayāna that awakens the capacity for primordial wisdom in a disciple and gives leave to practice. In 1.1 the yogin gives the child the empowerment of Guhyasamāja, and in part 4 a ruler asks the siddhas for one.

### emptiness

སྟོང་པ་ཉིད *stong pa nyid*; Sanskrit *śūnyatā*.

The lack of any fixed, independent being in anything, the illusory nature of all things. "Appearance-emptiness" is things appearing while empty, the two never apart; in 1.2 Tilopa asks the wisdom ḍākinī for it from the body's vault.

### eon

བསྐལ་པ *bskal pa*; Sanskrit *kalpa*.

The vast span of time in which a universe forms, lasts and ends. Tilopa's song in part 2 says that for ten million eons Tilopa has walked a hundred buddha fields.

### essence

སྙིང་པོ *snying po*.

Most likely the heart mantra, the essential mantra of a deity, which is recited to accomplish its speech. In 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī tells Tilopa to work at the essence for the ḍākinī's speech.

### experience and realization

ཉམས་རྟོགས *nyams rtogs*.

Experience is the passing states of meditation, such as bliss, clarity and stillness; realization is unchanging understanding of how things are. The opening says a teacher of the lineage must have both, with compassion and the rest.

### false notions

སྒྲོ་འདོགས *sgro 'dogs*; Sanskrit *samāropa*.

Superimpositions: the ideas the mind lays over things that aren't in them, and the doubts that come with them. The opening says the instruction of the nirmāṇakāya, the jewel of the lineage, clears them away by hearing and reflection.

### feast

ཚོགས་ཀྱི་འཁོར་ལོ *tshogs kyi 'khor lo*; Sanskrit *gaṇacakra*.

A gathering of the tantras in which practitioners offer food and drink, meat and wine among them, to the deities and enjoy them, to gather merit and mend their samayas. Tilopa finds Caryāpa among the ḍākinīs turning a feast in a charnel ground.

### fire at the end of time

དུས་མཐའི་མེ *dus mtha'i me*.

The fire at the end of the eon that burns up the universe. When the butcher in 1.4.6 refuses to kill, Tilopa's song answers, "burn: fire at the end of time".

### five families

རིགས་ལྔ *rigs lnga*.

The five buddha families, buddha, vajra, jewel, lotus and karma, each with its buddha, its wisdom and the poison of the mind it purifies. In 1.2 the ḍākinī of the five families, the saṃbhogakāya, keeps the three jewels in a palace of jewels.

### fold

བསྟན་པ *bstan pa*; Sanskrit *śāsana*.

The whole of a teacher's way, as a body that people enter and follow. At Nālandā in 1.4.2 the debaters agree that whoever loses must join the fold of the one who wins.

### fortune

སྐལ་བ *skal ba*.

One's karmic lot, the merit that makes a person fit for a Dharma; one "with the fortune" is a worthy recipient. A song in 1.2 says that at Gandhola, one with samaya and fortune takes the jewel and gets it.

### four transmissions

བཀའ་བབས་བཞི *bka' babs bzhi*.

The four lines of the Dharma Tilopa received: the illusory body from Nāgārjuna, dream from Caryāpa, luminosity from Lawapa and inner heat from the ḍākinī. Tilopa sings of them in 1.1 when asked who the guru was; the name Kagyü is sometimes read as their lineage.

### freed

གྲོལ་བ *grol ba*.

Liberated: released from saṃsāra, or in a moment realizing the nature of mind. Throughout the book, when Tilopa speaks or sings, all who hear are freed, from the people of the city in 1.1 to each of those Tilopa tames.

### free of thought

རྣམ་པར་མི་རྟོག་པ *rnam par mi rtog pa*; Sanskrit *nirvikalpa*.

Recognizing reality freed of the distortions of discursive thought, with no concepts laid over it. In 1.2 Tilopa asks for it from the mind's vault, as appearance-emptiness comes from the body's.

### fruition

འབྲས་བུ *'bras bu*; Sanskrit *phala*.

The result of the path, awakening itself; the same word is the fruit any act brings. With view, conduct and samaya it's one of the four things a practitioner holds. The opening calls Tilopa "the fruition itself made manifest", awakening in a human body.

### gandharva

དྲི་ཟ *dri za*; Sanskrit gandharva.

A celestial musician, a "scent eater" living on scents at the rim of Mount Meru, known for sweet song; the city of the gandharvas is a mirage. In 1.1 Nāgārjuna has gone to teach the ruler of the gandharvas.

### Gandhola

གྷནདོ་ལ *ghando la*; Sanskrit Gandhola.

The temple in Oḍḍiyāna where the ḍākinīs keep the jewels. Tilopa crosses its moat on a bridge of jewels, climbs its iron wall on a crystal ladder and opens its gate with a pair of grass mice to ask for the three wish-fulfilling jewels in 1.2. The manuscript also writes *ghando li*, *ghando lir*, *ghan do la*.

### genuine nature

གཉུག་མ *gnyug ma*; Sanskrit *nija*.

The mind's own nature, never made or put on, the "native" state, as against what's artificial. In 1.1 Tilopa wishes that the genuine nature arisen in Tilopa's mind enter the hearts of those who follow, and they're freed.

### Giver of Bliss

བདེ་སྟེར་མ *bde ster ma*.

The ḍākinī the ḍākinīs name as the child's true sister. In 1.1 the ḍākinīs tell the child his true family, and in 1.2 Tilopa tells the retinue at Gandhola that the Giver of Bliss has sent him.

### god

ལྷ *lha*; Sanskrit *deva*.

A being of the worlds of the gods: long-lived and happy but still mortal, still in saṃsāra, and no creator. In 1.1 the Brahmin who reads the marks wonders whether the newborn child is a god, and in 1.4.7 the Cārvāka is shown their worlds.

### great bliss

བདེ་བ་ཆེན་པོ *bde ba chen po*; Sanskrit *mahāsukha*.

The bliss of awakening as the tantras teach it, never apart from emptiness, not a pleasure that comes and goes. It's also a name of Cakrasaṃvara, whose Tibetan name means "supreme bliss", and the opening calls Tilopa great bliss in person.

### grounds and paths

ས་ལམ *sa lam*; Sanskrit *bhūmi* and *mārga*.

The ten grounds of the bodhisattva and the five paths, the stages by which most of the Dharma measures the way to buddhahood. The meaning of the vajra song of 1.1 says there are none to travel, and no fruition to gain.

### Guhyasamāja

གསང་བ་འདུས་པ *gsang ba 'dus pa*; Sanskrit Guhyasamāja.

A tantric deity and its tantra, "Assembly of Secrets", one of the major tantras of the new schools; its deity is a form of the buddha Akṣobhya. A yogin gives the child its empowerment in 1.1, and in part 4 Tilopa's song names it as his aggregates and elements.

### guru

བླ་མ *bla ma*; Sanskrit guru.

A spiritual teacher; in the tantras, the vajra teacher who gives empowerment, keeps the samayas, has experience and realization, and is regarded as a buddha. In 1.1 Tilopa names four human gurus, the four transmissions; in 1.3, in definitive meaning, Tilopa sings of having none.

### hand emblem

ཕྱག་མཚན *phyag mtshan*.

The object a deity holds, which identifies the deity and stands for its qualities, as a vajra or a skull cup. In 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī gives Tilopa one as the sign of the ḍākinī's mind.

### hell

དམྱལ་བ *dmyal ba*; Sanskrit *naraka*.

The lowest realms of saṃsāra, eight hot and eight cold, where a being is born by its own karma, not by judgment, and stays only until that karma is spent. In 1.4.7 the Cārvāka is shown the hells, and a song there says wrongdoing turns to hell in one's own mind.

### heroes

དཔའ་བོ *dpa' bo*, དཔའ་མོ *dpa' mo*; Sanskrit *vīra* and *vīriṇī*.

The heroes and heroines, the deities of a maṇḍala of both kinds, the ḍākas and the ḍākinīs. In 1.2 they sit in rows on either side of the wisdom ḍākinī of the dharmakāya, as Cakrasaṃvara's retinue rings that deity.

### Hevajra

ཧེ་བཛྲ *he badzra*; Sanskrit Hevajra.

A tantric deity and its tantra, a chief deity of the highest yoga tantra, much practiced by the new schools. The one who teaches the child in the temple gives him its empowerment in 1.1, and in part 4 Tilopa sings, "My body is Hevajra." The song of part 4 names them *kye'i rdo rje*.

### higher realms

མཐོ་རིས *mtho ris*.

Rebirth as a god or a human, as against the lower realms. A song in 1.4.7 says the virtue karma heaps turns to higher realms inside one's own mind.

### how things are

གནས་ལུགས *gnas lugs*.

The abiding nature of reality, things as they really are, beneath how they seem. In 1.2 Tilopa asks for it as a jewel from the mind's vault, and the ḍākinī answers that deep wisdom is its key.

### ignorance

མ་རིག་པ *ma rig pa*; Sanskrit *avidyā*.

Not recognizing the nature of mind and of things; the first of the twelve links of dependent arising and the root of saṃsāra. In Tilopa's song in 1.2, the lamp of wisdom clears it away.

### illusory body

སྒྱུ་ལུས *sgyu lus*; Sanskrit *māyādeha*.

The practice of knowing one's own body and all things as an illusion. It's one of the six dharmas of Nāropa and one of the four transmissions; Tilopa asked Nāgārjuna for it.

### innate

ལྷན་ཅིག་སྐྱེས་པ *lhan cig skyes pa*; Sanskrit *sahaja*.

The awakened nature that arises together with mind itself, "born together" with it, and was never apart from it. The vajra song of 1.1 says innate and primal wisdom lies hidden in the mind as oil lies in sesame.

### inner heat

གཏུམ་མོ *gtum mo*; Sanskrit *caṇḍālī*.

The yoga of kindling heat through the channels and winds until they enter the central channel and great bliss arises; the root of the six dharmas of Nāropa. Tilopa asked a ḍākinī for it, and it's one of the four transmissions.

### instruction

གདམས་ངག *gdams ngag*, མན་ངག *man ngag*; Sanskrit *upadeśa*.

A teacher's own practical Dharma on how to practice, passed by word of mouth, as against the scriptures themselves. Tilopa asks each guru for instruction, and the people ask Tilopa for it in turn; the book calls its three jewels instructions of the three bodies.

### introduction

ངོ་སྤྲོད *ngo sprod*.

The pointing-out instruction: the guru shows the disciple the nature of their own mind directly, as one introduces a person face to face. The guru who gives it is the disciple's root guru. It's the first of the three jewels the opening song names.

### the island of Sosa

སོ་ས་གླིང *so sa gling*.

Where the beer-seller of 1.4.4 lives on. A monkey and a cat Tilopa sent drank her beer on credit; Tilopa filled every jar again, and given the name Sunlight Lamp, the seller lives on there without dying.

### the island of the rākṣasas

སྲིན་པོའི་གླིང *srin po'i gling*; Sanskrit Laṅkā, the island of the rākṣasas.

Where the butcher of 1.4.6 lives on. Given the name Bliss-Making Joy after Tilopa turned them from killing, they live on in this land of the rākṣasas, the cannibal spirits, without dying.

### Jinabodhi

ཛི་ན་བྱང་ཆུབ *dzi na byang chub*; Sanskrit Jinabodhi.

The name given the Cārvāka of 1.4.7. The Cārvāka denied that karma bears fruit until Tilopa showed them the gods and the hells, and they live on at Śrīparvata without dying.

### Kalapa

ཀ་ལ་པ *ka la pa*.

Tilopa's name as a monk at the Sorrowless monastery. Tilopa's own family ordained him there in part 2, and the assembly brought Tilopa to account for killing small birds, which flew up alive.

### Kalpa Zangmo

སྐལ་བ་བཟང་མོ *skal ba bzang mo*.

The ḍākinī among the four human gurus, who gave Tilopa inner heat. Her name means "Fortunate Lady"; it's kept in Tibetan, since the text gives no Sanskrit for it.

### karma

ལས *las*; Sanskrit karma.

Action, and the working of cause and effect: every act bears its fruit for the one who did it. One who says karma bears no fruit denies that, and with it any reason for virtue; in 1.4.7 Tilopa tames a Cārvāka who said so.

### karma ḍākinī

ལས་ཀྱི་མཁའ་འགྲོ *las kyi mkha' 'gro*; Sanskrit *karmaḍākinī*.

A ḍākinī who carries out awakened activity, as against the wisdom ḍākinī; karma here is that activity, not what one does and its fruit. In 1.2 the karma ḍākinī keeps the gate, gives siddhis to the faithful and devours those whose samayas are broken.

### Kasori

ཀ་སོ་རི *ka so ri*.

One of the three the wisdom ḍākinī tells Tilopa to take into his care at the monastery of the Crown Jewel, with Nāro and Riri, at the end of 1.2. Nothing more is told of Kasori in this life.

### Kereli

ཀེ་རེ་ལི *ke re li*.

The charnel ground where Tilopa and Bharima practice the conduct of the Secret Mantra to its end. They go there from the market of Pañcapana, and back in the market Tilopa wins the siddhi close to Mahāmudrā.

### Khasu

ཁ་སུ *kha su*.

A river in the east of India whose source is on the bank of the Dukata. There, in the charnel ground of the Masha Grove, stands the Sorrowless monastery where Tilopa is ordained in part 2.

### Kimitsikili

ཀི་མི་ཙི་ཀི་ལི *ki mi tsi ki li*.

Where the sorcerer of 1.4.8 lives on. A sorcerer killed everyone with black magic until Tilopa made them like an old tree stump; given the name Nyiimi, they live on at Kimitsikili.

### kye ho

ཀྱེ་ཧོ *kye ho*.

A cry of amazement that also calls to the hearers, "O!" or "Behold!", and often opens a song of realization. It's near *e ma ho*, "How marvelous!", which only marvels and calls no one. The vajra song of 1.1 cries it as it turns to what it means.

### kye ma

ཀྱེ་མ *kye ma*.

A cry of surprise and grief, "Alas!" It calls to the hearers as *kye ho* does, but in grief, not in wonder. The ḍākinīs cry it in 1.2 when Tilopa overwhelms them: as the lamp destroys the moth, they say, those who'd eat Tilopa are destroyed.

### Lawapa

ལ་ཝ་པ *la wa pa*; Sanskrit Lavapa, Kambala.

One of the four human gurus, who gave Tilopa luminosity, and who slept twelve years at a crossroads. Also called Kambalapāda, they were named for the woolen blanket they wore. The manuscript also writes *la ba pa*.

### lineage

བརྒྱུད་པ *brgyud pa*.

The unbroken line through which a Dharma passes, each holder receiving it from the one before. "The jewel of the lineage" is the account of that line, and this book, beginning with Tilopa's life, is the first part of that account.

### logic

ཚད་མ *tshad ma*; Sanskrit *pramāṇa*.

Valid cognition, the Buddhist science of reasoning and knowledge, the means by which a thing is known for certain. In 1.4.1 Tilopa and the Unrivaled debate by its rules, and come out equal.

### lotus-born

ཆུ་སྐྱེས *chu skyes*.

A poet's word for the lotus, in Tibetan "born of water", since a lotus rises from a lake. The opening says the lotus-born came from the lake of the three bodies, and means Prajñābhadra, who's Tilopa; it isn't Padmasambhava's name here.

### lowest caste

གདོལ་པ *gdol pa*; Sanskrit *mātaṅga*.

Those who stood below the four classes of Indian society, shunned by the others as impure. In 1.1 the yogin who teaches the child sits in such a hut, having set purity and impurity aside.

### Lūhipa

ལུ་ཧི་པ *lu hi pa*; Sanskrit Lūipa.

The mahāsiddha whose meaning the ḍākinī teaches Tilopa in the temple. A mahāsiddha of the tenth century, the first of the eighty-four in Abhayadatta's list, who ate fish guts to purify his pride.

### luminosity

འོད་གསལ *'od gsal*; Sanskrit *prabhāsvara*.

The bright, knowing nature of mind, also called "clear light". As one of the six dharmas of Nāropa it's the practice of recognizing it, above all in deep sleep. Tilopa asked Lawapa for it, and it's one of the four transmissions.

### Mahāmāyā

མ་ཧཱ་མ་ཡ *ma hā ma ya*; Sanskrit Mahāmāyā.

A tantric deity and its tantra, "Great Illusion", one of the mother tantras of the highest yoga tantra. In the song of part 4 Tilopa sings, "my speech is Mahāmāyā."

### Mahāmudrā

ཕྱག་རྒྱ་ཆེན་པོ *phyag rgya chen po*; Sanskrit mahāmudrā.

The nature of mind and the practice that realizes it. Lawapa won its siddhi sleeping at a crossroads; the wisdom ḍākinī tells Tilopa to practice it for his mind, and Tilopa wins the siddhi close to it.

### Maheśvara

དབང་ཕྱུག་ཆེན་པོ *dbang phyug chen po*; Sanskrit Maheśvara.

The great god, Śiva, who blessed the charnel ground of Sala Bhehara. The eight great charnel grounds are said to have grown up where the parts of Rudra's body fell.

### maṇḍala

དཀྱིལ་འཁོར *dkyil 'khor*; Sanskrit maṇḍala.

Either of two things: a deity's palace and circle of deities, drawn in colored sand or imagined, into which a disciple is led at empowerment; or the whole universe, imagined and set out as an offering to a guru. The book has both kinds.

### Maṅgalam

བཀྲ་ཤིས *bkra shis*; Sanskrit maṅgalam.

Sanskrit for "auspiciousness", spoken as a blessing at the close of a work. Marpa ends the book with it, after the colophon that says it was written down at Drowolung for Dode.

### māra

བདུད *bdud*; Sanskrit māra.

A being that harms practice; Māra, who tried to keep the Buddha from awakening, stands for a practitioner's own clinging to a self. In 1.2 the retinue says Tilopa is fooled by one, and Tilopa answers that the ḍākinī is no māra.

### marks

མཚན *mtshan*, མཚན་མ *mtshan ma*.

The signs on a body that tell what a being is. A Brahmin who reads the marks reads them in a newborn child, as one reads Tilopa's in 1.1; the old ones "with the marks" bear a ḍākinī's signs, ugliness, the breath and the limping gait.

### the Masha Grove

རྨ་ཤའི་ཚལ *rma sha'i tshal*.

The charnel ground of the Sorrowless monastery. It lies at the source of the river Khasu, on the bank of the Dukata in the east of India, and there Tilopa is ordained and named Kalapa.

### Melodious Silent One

དབྱངས་ལྡན་ལྐུགས་པ *dbyangs ldan lkugs pa*.

The name given the singer of 1.4.5. A skilled singer challenged Tilopa to a contest of songs; Tilopa's song had no end, and the singer lives on in Nagara.

### Mighty Intellect

ནུས་ལྡན་བློ་གྲོས *nus ldan blo gros*.

The name given the yogin of 1.4.1. The yogin called the Unrivaled offered to lead the ruler's feast; Tilopa matched and then outdid them in debate and magic, and they live on in Oḍḍiyāna.

### mind itself

སེམས་ཉིད *sems nyid*; Sanskrit *cittatā*.

The nature of mind, a name for buddha nature, as against the thoughts that pass through it. Tilopa's song in 1.2 calls it never born, and a song there makes it one with the nature of things and the dharmakāya.

### the monastery of the Crown Jewel

གཙུག་གི་ནོར་བུའི་དགོན་པ *gtsug gi nor bu'i dgon pa*.

Where the ḍākinī sends Tilopa; its temple is Aḍona. At the end of 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī tells Tilopa to go there and take Nāro, Riri and Kasori into his care, and Tilopa goes, singing as he leaves Gandhola.

### nāga

ཀླུ *klu*; Sanskrit nāga.

A serpent spirit of the waters and the underworld, one of the beings the Brahmin who reads the marks thinks the newborn Tilopa may be.

### Nagara

ན་ག་ར *na ga ra*; Sanskrit Nagara.

Where the singer lives on. The singer of 1.4.5, given the name Melodious Silent One after Tilopa's song had no end, lives on there without dying, as the book tells it.

### Nāgārjuna

ཀླུ་སྒྲུབ *klu sgrub*; Sanskrit Nāgārjuna.

One of Tilopa's four human gurus, who gave Tilopa the illusory body; here the tantric teacher of that name. The great teacher who founded the Madhyamaka is also counted among the eighty-four mahāsiddhas and stands in the Guhyasamāja tradition.

### Nālandā

ནཱ་ལན་དཱ *nā lan dā*; Sanskrit Nālandā.

The great monastery where Tilopa tames the non-Buddhist; "glorious Nālandā", as the manuscript writes it, *dpal shri na len tra*. It was the largest of the monastic universities of India, in today's Bihar.

### Nāropa

ནཱ་རོ་པ *nā ro pa*; Sanskrit Nāropa, Nāḍapāda.

Tilopa's disciple, the great paṇḍita, who went through twelve hardships under Tilopa; Marpa's guru. The homage calls Nāropa *nā ro'i paṇ chen*, "the great paṇḍita Nāropa", and the wisdom ḍākinī *na ro*, "Nāro", with Riri and Kasori.

### nature of mind

སེམས་ཀྱི་ངོ་བོ *sems kyi ngo bo*.

What mind is in itself, beneath its thoughts; word for word, "the essence of mind". To see it is the aim of the pointing-out instruction and of Mahāmudrā. The child first sees it after the empowerment of Guhyasamāja in 1.1.

### nature of things

ཆོས་ཉིད *chos nyid*; Sanskrit *dharmatā*.

The ultimate nature of all that is, emptiness. A song in 1.2 makes it one with mind itself and the dharmakāya: "mind itself, the nature of things, dharmakāya".

### nine cycles

ལུས་མེད་མཁའ་འགྲོའི་ཆོས་སྐོར་དགུ *lus med mkha' 'gro'i chos skor dgu*.

The nine cycles of the Dharma of the bodiless ḍākinīs, given in the nine lines that follow them in 1.2. Tilopa receives them on the road out of the sky, from ḍākinīs without bodies.

### nine moods of the dance

གར་གྱི་ཉམས་དགུ *gar gyi nyams dgu*; Sanskrit *rasa*.

The nine moods the wrathful deities of the tantras show: charming, heroic and repulsive in body; laughing, fierce and terrifying in speech; compassionate, majestic and peaceful in mind. The opening says the three bodies ripen those to be trained in them.

### nirmāṇakāya

སྤྲུལ་སྐུ *sprul sku*; Sanskrit nirmāṇakāya.

The third of the three bodies: a form that appears to beings both pure and impure, to tame them, as a buddha, an artisan, a musician or anything that'll help. Tilopa's life is told as a nirmāṇakāya's; in Tibet the word also names an incarnate lama.

### non-Buddhist

མུ་སྟེགས་པ *mu stegs pa*; Sanskrit *tīrthika*.

A teacher or follower of one of India's other schools, Hindu, Jain or materialist. In 1.4.2 Tilopa tames a non-Buddhist teacher at Nālandā by debate and by power.

### non-duality

གཉིས་སུ་མེད་པ *gnyis su med pa*; Sanskrit *advaya*.

The absence of two: of subject and object, saṃsāra and nirvāṇa, bliss and emptiness. In 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī of the dharmakāya lives in non-duality, dwelling in the manner of not dwelling.

### Nyiimi

ཉི་ཨི་མི *nyi i mi*.

The name given the sorcerer of 1.4.8, as the Tibetan writes it. A sorcerer was killing everyone with black magic; Tilopa outdid them and made them like an old tree stump, and they live on at Kimitsikili.

### Oḍḍiyāna

ཨུ་རྒྱན *u rgyan*; Sanskrit Oḍḍiyāna, Uḍḍiyāna.

The land of the ḍākinīs in the west. There, in the temple of Gandhola, Tilopa asks the ḍākinīs for the three wish-fulfilling jewels in 1.2. Many scholars put it in the Swat valley of today's Pakistan.

### one taste

རོ་སྙོམས *ro snyoms*; Sanskrit *samarasa*.

The realization that everything has the same taste, the nature of mind; also "even taste". As *ro gcig* it's the third of the four yogas of Mahāmudrā. One of the nine cycles in 1.2 calls it the outer mirror.

### outer, inner and secret

ཕྱི་ནང་གསང་གསུམ *phyi nang gsang gsum*.

The three levels at which the Dharma is given, each deeper and more hidden than the last. The opening sets its three jewels on them, outwardly, inwardly and secretly, matching the three bodies from the nirmāṇakāya outside to the dharmakāya within.

### outsiders and insiders

ཕྱི་རོལ་པ *phyi rol pa*, ནང་པ *nang pa*.

The non-Buddhists and the Buddhists, those outside the Dharma and those inside it. In 1.4.2 all of them, outsiders and insiders, had to rise before the non-Buddhist teacher at Nālandā.

### painted card

ཙ་ཀ་ལི *tsa ka li*.

A small painting of a deity, a maṇḍala or an offering, used in rites and empowerments. In 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī gives Tilopa one as the sign of the ḍākinī's body, with a syllable and a hand emblem.

### Pañcapana

པཎ་ཙ་པ་ན *paṇ tsa pa na*.

The name the ḍākinīs give the child in their song, and the name Tilopa gives himself at the palace of the ḍākinīs; nearly the name of the market town in Bengal, *paṇ tsha pa na*, where he serves Bharima. The text doesn't explain it.

### paṇḍita

མཁས་པ *mkhas pa*; Sanskrit paṇḍita.

A scholar of the Buddhist Dharma and the sciences of India, such as the great teachers of its monastic universities. In the book the paṇḍitas stand against the siddhas and simple yogins, and Nāropa is called the great paṇḍita.

### path of freedom

གྲོལ་ལམ *grol lam*.

The practice, after empowerment, of the guru's oral instruction, above all resting in the natural state of mind, Mahāmudrā. With the path of ripening it makes "ripening and freeing"; the opening calls the instruction of the dharmakāya its jewel.

### path of ripening

སྨིན་ལམ *smin lam*.

The empowerments, which ripen a disciple's being with the capacity to realize the bodies of a buddha. With the path of freedom it makes "ripening and freeing"; the opening calls the instruction of the saṃbhogakāya its wish-fulfilling jewel.

### person of illusion

སྒྱུ་མའི་སྐྱེས་བུ *sgyu ma'i skyes bu*.

A phantom, a person conjured in a magical illusion, a stock image of the Dharma for beings that seem to live and are empty. In 1.4.3 Tilopa sings that where illusion's armies fall there's no wrongdoing, and tells the hearer to kill the person of illusion.

### Prajñābhadra

ཤེས་རབ་བཟང་པོ *shes rab bzang po*; Sanskrit Prajñābhadra.

Tilopa's name given by the ḍākinīs in Oḍḍiyāna (1.2), meaning excellent wisdom; "Tilo Prajñābhadra" for *te lo shes rab bzang po*. The homage of part 0 bows to Prajñābhadra, and Tilopa sings the name as the bird of the mind takes wing.

### Prajñāpāramitā

ཤེས་རབ་ཀྱི་ཕ་རོལ་ཏུ་ཕྱིན་པ *shes rab kyi pha rol tu phyin pa*; Sanskrit Prajñāpāramitā.

The "perfection of wisdom": the sūtras of the Mahāyāna that teach emptiness, the longest in a hundred thousand lines. In 1.1 it's being recited in a temple when a stranger comes and asks the reciter what it means.

### preceptor and teacher

མཁན་སློབ *mkhan slob*; Sanskrit *upādhyāya* and *ācārya*.

The two who give ordination: the preceptor, who gives the vows, and the teacher, who guides the newly ordained. At the Sorrowless monastery in part 2, Tilopa's own family are Tilopa's preceptor and teacher.

### primordial wisdom

ཡེ་ཤེས *ye shes*; Sanskrit *jñāna*.

The timeless awareness of the awakened mind, present from the beginning, never made or gained; the songs sometimes call it primal wisdom. It's told apart from wisdom, the knowing that discerns. The vajra song of 1.1 says it lies innate in the mind.

### prophecy

ལུང་བསྟན *lung bstan*.

A foretelling, by a buddha, a deity or a ḍākinī, that tells a practitioner where to go and what to do. Tilopa's life moves from one prophecy to the next: the ḍākinīs send the child to a charnel ground to find a guru.

### Protector of Beings

འགྲོ་བའི་མགོན་པོ *'gro ba'i mgon po*; Sanskrit *Jagannātha*.

A title of a buddha, or of a teacher seen as one, who shelters beings from the suffering of saṃsāra. The opening gives it to Prajñābhadra, Tilopa's given name, in the homage before the life begins.

### provisional meaning

དྲང་དོན *drang don*; Sanskrit *neyārtha*.

A meaning that leads somewhere and needs interpreting, as against the definitive meaning. In provisional meaning the book tells Tilopa's life as though Tilopa were an ordinary human who needed gurus, prophesied to by ḍākinīs and practicing.

### provost

དགེ་སྐོས *dge skos*.

The officer who keeps discipline in a monastery. In part 2 the provost of the Sorrowless monastery calls an assembly and brings Tilopa to account for killing birds.

### Rāhu

སྒྲ་གཅན *sgra gcan*; Sanskrit Rāhu.

The asura who swallows the sun and moon in eclipse; "Rāhu the asura" for *lha min sgra gcan*. In the opening song the whispered lineage is the sun's light, which dreads to hear Rāhu named, as the sun dreads eclipse.

### rākṣasa

སྲིན་པོ *srin po*; Sanskrit rākṣasa.

A cannibal spirit, one of the eight classes of gods and spirits; their land is Laṅkā. In 1.4.6 the butcher Tilopa tames, named Bliss-Making Joy, lives on the island of the rākṣasas without dying.

### reference point

དམིགས་པ *dmigs pa*; Sanskrit *ālambana*.

The mind's fixing on an object as this or that, its support. In 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī of the dharmakāya lives "where every reference point has ended", where the mind grasps at nothing at all.

### remedy

གཉེན་པོ *gnyen po*; Sanskrit *pratipakṣa*.

A practice that counters a fault, as patience counters anger; an antidote. Where everything is the innate, the meaning of the vajra song of 1.1 says, there's no remedy to apply and nothing to give up.

### Riri

རི་རི *ri ri*.

One of the three Tilopa is told to take into his care, with Nāro and Kasori. Nothing more is told of Riri in this life; the wisdom ḍākinī names them at the end of 1.2.

### rite of the ground

ས་ཆོག *sa chog*.

The rite that takes hold of the site where a maṇḍala will be made, before any other rite is done there. In 1.4.1 the paṇḍitas the ruler has invited perform it and the rest, and it's proclaimed that whatever they do will succeed.

### root tantra

རྩ་རྒྱུད *rtsa rgyud*; Sanskrit *mūlatantra*.

The chief scripture of a cycle, which explanatory tantras explain. In 1.2 the ḍākinīs give Tilopa the root tantra of Cakrasaṃvara in fifty-one chapters, with its explanatory tantras and the whispered lineage.

### sacred substance

དམ་རྫས *dam rdzas*; Sanskrit *samayadravya*.

A substance blessed by mantra and taken as a samaya, such as the ambrosia of a feast, so that taking it keeps the samaya. One of the nine cycles in 1.2 calls it the key of insight.

### Sala Bhehara

ས་ལ་བྷེ་ཧ་ར *sa la bhe ha ra*.

A charnel ground in the south, blessed by Maheśvara. In 1.1 the ḍākinīs send the child there to find a guru, and there the child meets Caryāpa, Lawapa and the yogin sent in Nāgārjuna's place.

### Salden

གསལ་ལྡན *gsal ldan*.

Tilopa's mother, a Brahmin. She lives in Dzako, and when the ugly ones with the marks appear, Salden fears for the child and asks what will help. The text also writes *gsal ldan ma*.

### Saldrön

གསལ་སྒྲོན *gsal sgron*.

Tilopa's sister, a Brahmin. She lives in Dzako with the family, and the child names her in his song to the ḍākinīs in 1.1. The text also writes *gsal ba'i sgron ma*.

### Salö

གསལ་འོད *gsal 'od*.

Tilopa's name as a child, "Clear Light", given because the east of India filled with light when he was born. It's the name the child gives in the song of 1.1.

### Salwa

གསལ་བ *gsal ba*.

Tilopa's father, a Brahmin of Dzako. The family prayed at every shrine for a child, and in 1.2 Salwa finds the crystal ladder, the bridge of jewels and the grass mice.

### samaya

དམ་ཚིག *dam tshig*; Sanskrit samaya.

A commitment of the Vajrayāna taken at empowerment: outwardly to keep harmony with the guru and one's companions, inwardly never to stray from the practice. In 1.2 the karma ḍākinī cuts down those whose samayas are broken.

### saṃbhogakāya

ལོངས་སྐུ *longs sku*; Sanskrit saṃbhogakāya.

The second of the three bodies: a body of light adorned with the marks of a buddha, which appears only to bodhisattvas, and from which the nirmāṇakāya arises. In the palace of 1.2 it's the rank of the ḍākinī of the five families.

### saṃsāra

འཁོར་བ *'khor ba*; Sanskrit saṃsāra.

The round of birth and death among the six classes of beings, "circling", driven by karma and ignorance. A song in 1.4.8 tells the listener to look into their own mind, cut saṃsāra loose and leave.

### scriptural authority

ལུང *lung*; Sanskrit *āgama*.

The word of the Buddha and the authoritative treatises handed down. In part 3 the ruler says a verse on giving and receiving must not go against it, nor against logic or instruction.

### Secret Mantra

གསང་སྔགས *gsang sngags*; Sanskrit *guhyamantra*.

Another name for the Vajrayāna, the path of the tantras, secret because it's given only to those who've received empowerment. At Kereli, Tilopa and Bharima practice its conduct together and bring it to its end.

### secret word

མཁའ་འགྲོའི་གསང་ཚིག *mkha' 'gro'i gsang tshig*.

The hidden, symbolic speech of the ḍākinīs, the code in which their secret Dharma is kept, so that only one who holds its key can open it. The opening song calls the whispered lineage the ḍākinīs' own secret word.

### self-aware

རང་རིག *rang rig*.

Knowing itself. With self-born and self-luminous, it describes primordial wisdom: it knows itself, occurs of itself, and is clear by its own nature. Tilopa's song in 1.2 calls mind "self-aware, self-born and clear".

### self-freed

རང་གྲོལ *rang grol*.

Freed by itself: a thought or feeling that unravels of its own accord with no remedy applied. A song in 1.2 says Mahāmudrā dawns self-freed, and one of the nine cycles says to look at it so.

### siddha

གྲུབ་ཐོབ *grub thob*; Sanskrit siddha.

A yogin of the tantras who has won siddhi, the supreme or the common. The greatest are the mahāsiddhas, and Tilopa is counted among the eighty-four. The book sets the siddhas against the paṇḍitas, the scholars.

### siddhi

དངོས་གྲུབ *dngos grub*; Sanskrit siddhi.

What practice attains. The common siddhis are worldly powers, such as clairvoyance and flight; the supreme siddhi is awakening, Mahāmudrā. Lawapa wins it sleeping at a crossroads, and in 1.2 the karma ḍākinī gives siddhis to the faithful.

### signs

བརྡ *brda*.

The gestures, symbols and words by which a teacher shows what words can't say. Only as convention, in signs, can a guru show the meaning, and in 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī answers Tilopa in signs of body, speech and mind.

### Siṃhacandra

སེང་གེ་ཟླ་བ *seng ge zla ba*; Sanskrit Siṃhacandra.

The ruler who invites the siddhas in part 4. The ruler asks for empowerment, and Tilopa raises a maṇḍala of colored powders in the sky that each sees differently.

### simple yogin

ཀུ་ས་ལི *ku sa li*; Sanskrit *kusāli*.

A yogin of the simplest life, a beggar who does what comes naturally, as against the learned paṇḍitas. In 1.4.1, while the paṇḍitas perform the rites, an ugly stranger with the marks comes to the simple yogins and asks who'll lead their feast.

### Śītavana

སིལ་བའི་ཚལ *sil ba'i tshal*; Sanskrit Śītavana.

A charnel ground near Bodh Gaya, the first of the eight great ones; its name means "Cool Grove". The non-Buddhist Tilopa tames at Nālandā in 1.4.2, given the name Black Virtue, lives on there without dying.

### Sorrowless

མྱ་ངན་མེད་པ *mya ngan med pa*.

The monastery where Tilopa is ordained. It stands in the charnel ground of the Masha Grove, by the source of the river Khasu, where Tilopa is named Kalapa and brought to account.

### Śrīparvata

དཔལ་གྱི་རི *dpal gyi ri*; Sanskrit Śrīparvata.

The mountain where Jinabodhi lives on. A mountain in the south of India where Nāgārjuna spent his last days; the Cārvāka of 1.4.7 lives on there without dying.

### stage of completion

རྫོགས་རིམ *rdzogs rim*; Sanskrit *utpannakrama*.

The second of the two stages: the dissolving of the image and the yogas of the channels, winds and drops of the subtle body. In 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī tells Tilopa to work at it, Mahāmudrā, for the ḍākinī's mind.

### stage of generation

བསྐྱེད་རིམ *bskyed rim*; Sanskrit *utpattikrama*.

The first of the two stages: the whole meditation from dissolving into emptiness to the full image of the maṇḍala and its deities. In 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī tells Tilopa to work at it to accomplish the ḍākinī's body.

### suchness

དེ་ཉིད *de nyid*; Sanskrit *tattva*.

Things just as they are, "thatness", another name for emptiness and the nature of things. The vajra song of 1.1 says that leaning on the guru, suchness shows itself in its signs, as oil comes out of sesame.

### Sugata

བདེ་བར་གཤེགས་པ *bde bar gshegs pa*; Sanskrit sugata.

A buddha, "the Blissfully Gone", gone to bliss by a blissful path. In 1.1 the two stages Tilopa trains in are called the essence of all the paths that shine in the undivided mind of the Sugatas.

### Sunlight Lamp

ཉི་འོད་སྒྲོན་མ *nyi 'od sgron ma*.

The name given the beer-seller of 1.4.4. A monkey and a cat Tilopa sent drank a beer-seller's beer on credit; Tilopa filled every jar again, and the seller lives on the island of Sosa without dying.

### syllable

ཡིག་འབྲུ *yig 'bru*; Sanskrit *akṣara*.

A letter; as the sign of a deity's speech, the seed syllable from which a deity arises in the stage of generation. In 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī gives Tilopa one as the sign of the ḍākinī's speech.

### tantra

རྒྱུད *rgyud*; Sanskrit tantra.

A scripture of the Vajrayāna, read as the continuity of ground, path and fruition; to explain a tantra is to teach it. The yogin explains the tantra of Guhyasamāja to the child, and the ḍākinīs give Tilopa the root tantra of Cakrasaṃvara.

### this one life and this one body

ཚེ་གཅིག་ལུས་གཅིག *tshe gcig lus gcig*.

The promise of the tantras that buddhahood can be won within a single lifetime, not over countless lives. The opening asks it of a disciple of the whispered lineage: one able to seek buddhahood in their own mind in this one life.

### those to be trained

གདུལ་བྱ *gdul bya*; Sanskrit *vineya*.

The beings a buddha or a teacher can tame and bring to awakening, those a Dharma is meant for. The opening says the three bodies ripen them; the tamings of part 1.4 show Tilopa doing that work, one person at a time.

### thousand buddhas

བསྐལ་པ་བཟང་པོ *bskal pa bzang po*.

The thousand buddhas who appear in this "good eon", the Buddha of history being the fourth. Tilopa's song in part 2 says Tilopa has seen them all.

### three bodies

སྐུ་གསུམ *sku gsum*; Sanskrit *trikāya*.

The three ways a buddha is present: the dharmakāya, awakened mind itself, empty and without form; the saṃbhogakāya, a body of light seen only by beings of great realization; and the nirmāṇakāya, the form a buddha takes in the world. The book opens on them, and tells Tilopa's life as the life of a nirmāṇakāya.

### three jewels

རྣམ་གསུམ་ནོར་བུ *rnam gsum nor bu*.

The three wish-fulfilling jewels of this cycle of the Dharma, named in the opening song: introduction, dream and the lineage. They aren't the Three Jewels all Buddhists take refuge in, the Buddha, the Dharma and the Saṅgha.

### three realms

ཁམས་གསུམ *khams gsum*; Sanskrit *tridhātu*.

The desire realm, the form realm and the formless realm: the whole of saṃsāra. A song in 1.4.2 boasts of the power to make the three realms shake.

### three times

དུས་གསུམ *dus gsum*.

Past, present and future, all of time; the buddhas of the three times are every buddha who has been, is and will be. Part 4 shows Tilopa as the body in which all of them gather.

### three wheels

འཁོར་ལོ་གསུམ *'khor lo gsum*.

The three works of a monastic life: study, meditation and work. In part 2 the others at the Sorrowless monastery give themselves to them, and Tilopa doesn't keep the Dharma at all.

### Tilopa

ཏེ་ལོ་པ *te lo pa*; Sanskrit Tilopa, Tillipa.

The mahāsiddha whose life this is, 988–1069 by the usual reckoning; the first of the Kagyü lineage, Nāropa's guru. The text also writes *ti lo pa*, *te lo*, *te los*, *ti lo pa'i*; "Tilo" where the Tibetan has *te lo* and the line wants it.

### Til Sungzhab

ཏིལ་བསྲུངས་ཞབས *til bsrungs zhabs*.

"The Sesame Keeper", the Tibetan the text gives for the name Tilopa, which he won pounding sesame in the market of Pañcapana. Til is Tibetan for sesame.

### transference

འཕོ་བ *'pho ba*; Sanskrit *saṃkrānti*.

The ejection of consciousness through the crown of the head to a buddha field at death; one of the six dharmas of Nāropa. Tilopa's song to the butcher in 1.4.6 teaches it as killing beyond thought.

### tree of awakening

བྱང་ཆུབ་ཤིང *byang chub shing*; Sanskrit *bodhivṛkṣa*.

The fig tree at Bodh Gaya under which the Buddha awoke, and any place where awakening is won. In the ḍākinīs' prophecy of 1.1 it's where the child is told to herd experience, not buffalo.

### twelve great hardships

དཀའ་བ་བཅུ་གཉིས *dka' ba bcu gnyis*.

The twelve trials Tilopa put Nāropa through before giving Nāropa the Dharma. Nāropa had left a great seat of learning to seek Tilopa, and the homage at the opening honors Nāropa as the one who went through them.

### two stages

རིམ་གཉིས *rim gnyis*; Sanskrit *krama*.

The stage of generation, in which one imagines oneself as the deity, and the stage of completion, in which one dissolves the image and works with the subtle body; together, the meditation of the tantras. Tilopa trains in them while locked up in 1.1.

### the ultimate

དོན་དམ *don dam*; Sanskrit *paramārtha*.

Ultimate truth, the nature of things as they are, as against convention, the conventional truth of everyday appearance. Tilopa's song in 1.4.1 says Tilo saw the ultimate.

### the Unrivaled

འགྲན་ཟླ་མེད་པ *'gran zla med pa*.

The yogin of 1.4.1, whose name means "without rival". They offered to lead the ruler's feast, but Tilopa outdid them; given the name Mighty Intellect, they live on in Oḍḍiyāna.

### Vajrabhairava

རྡོ་རྗེ་འཇིགས་བྱེད *rdo rje 'jigs byed*; Sanskrit Vajrabhairava.

A wrathful tantric deity, "Vajra Terror", a form of Yamāntaka with a buffalo's face and many arms and legs; its tantra belongs to the father tantras. Tilopa's song in part 4 names it as his lesser limbs.

### vajra song

རྡོ་རྗེའི་མགུར *rdo rje'i mgur*.

A song of realization sung by a teacher; a vajra, Indra's weapon and the diamond, is the sign of what can't be broken. Tilopa and Bharima sing one together in 1.1 before the ruler, and the book holds many more of Tilopa's.

### Vajravārāhī

རྡོ་རྗེ་ཕག་མོ *rdo rje phag mo*; Sanskrit Vajravārāhī.

Cakrasaṃvara's partner in union, named as the child's true mother. "Vajra Sow", a wisdom ḍākinī shown red with a sow's head beside her own, the root of all the ḍākinīs' emanations.

### vase breathing

བུམ་པ་ཅན *bum pa can*; Sanskrit *kumbhaka*.

The holding of the breath in yoga, filling the belly like a vase. A song of Tilopa's in 1.4.2 tells one who's sinking to fill the vase with wind, and says Tilo's free from every fear.

### victory banner

རྒྱལ་མཚན *rgyal mtshan*.

One of the eight auspicious symbols, the banner of the victory of the Dharma. The teacher's song in 1.4.7 tells one who feels low to face the top of the victory banner.

### view

ལྟ་བ *lta ba*; Sanskrit *dṛṣṭi*.

One's understanding of how things are. With conduct, fruition and samaya it makes the whole of a practitioner's way, and Tilopa names all four together when the retinue asks who Tilopa is in 1.2.

### virtue

དགེ་བ *dge ba*; Sanskrit *kuśala*.

An act that brings happiness to the one who does it, the wholesome; the opposite of wrongdoing. The Cārvāka of 1.4.7 says there's neither virtue nor wrongdoing, and the debate turns on it.

### whispered lineage

སྙན་བརྒྱུད *snyan brgyud*.

Dharma passed only by word of mouth, from a teacher's lips to a disciple's ear. This one, the hearing lineage of Cakrasaṃvara, came from the ḍākinīs to Tilopa, then through Nāropa and Marpa to Milarepa and Rechungpa. The book tells how Tilopa won it in Oḍḍiyāna.

### wind-mind

རླུང་སེམས *rlung sems*.

The mind and the wind it rides, closely joined in the subtle body. Tilopa's song in 1.4.6 tells the butcher to draw the wind-mind out through the crown of the head.

### wisdom

ཤེས་རབ *shes rab*; Sanskrit *prajñā*.

The knowing that discerns things as they are, the sixth perfection, told apart from primordial wisdom, the timeless knowing of the awakened mind. In 1.2 the ḍākinī's song makes deep wisdom the key to the jewel of how things are.

### wisdom ḍākinī

ཡེ་ཤེས་མཁའ་འགྲོ་མ *ye shes mkha' 'gro ma*; Sanskrit *jñānaḍākinī*.

A fully awakened being in a pure female form, as against the worldly ḍākinīs, who are spirits. In 1.2 the wisdom ḍākinī of the dharmakāya rules in Oḍḍiyāna, ringed by heroes and heroines, and gives Tilopa the signs and the whispered lineage.

### wish-fulfilling jewel

ཡིད་བཞིན་ནོར་བུ *yid bzhin nor bu*; Sanskrit *cintāmaṇi*.

The gem of Indian legend that grants every wish; the Buddha, the guru and the nature of mind are all called by its name. The book keeps the whispered lineage as three such jewels, and this life of Tilopa belongs to the first, the jewel of the lineage.

### without dying

འདས་གྲོངས་མེད་པར *'das grongs med par*.

Never dying. Each of those Tilopa tames in 1.4 is given a name and lives on in a sacred place without dying, "even now", as the yogin lives in Oḍḍiyāna.

### worthy vessels

སྣོད་ལྡན *snod ldan*.

Disciples open and fit to receive the Dharma, as a sound vessel holds what's poured into it. In 1.2 Tilopa sings of serving worthy vessels well, as the chief of the ḍākinīs had prophesied.

### wrongdoing

སྡིག་པ *sdig pa*; Sanskrit *pāpa*.

A harmful act, which brings suffering to the one who does it; the opposite of virtue. The Cārvāka of 1.4.7 says there's neither virtue nor wrongdoing, and Tilopa's song in 1.4.3 says that where there's no mind there's none.

### yakṣa

གནོད་སྦྱིན *gnod sbyin*; Sanskrit yakṣa.

A spirit of wealth and of wild places, powerful and quick to harm, one of the beings the Brahmin who reads the marks thinks the newborn Tilopa may be.

### yogic discipline

བརྟུལ་ཞུགས *brtul zhugs*; Sanskrit *vrata*.

The unconventional conduct that advanced practitioners of the tantras take up to deepen their practice and train their awareness. The yogin in the hut keeps it, and Tilopa's show of losing his mind in 1.1 is such conduct.

### yogic discipline of awareness

རིག་པ་བརྟུལ་ཞུགས *rig pa brtul zhugs*; Sanskrit *vidyāvrata*.

Yogic discipline carried by awareness. In 1.2 Tilopa overwhelms the ḍākinīs with it, resting unmoved in body, speech and mind: the child's body doesn't move, the speech doesn't falter, the mind doesn't shrink.

### yogin

རྣལ་འབྱོར་པ *rnal 'byor pa*; Sanskrit yogin.

A practitioner of yoga; in the book, a practitioner of the tantras who lives apart, given to meditation, often in a charnel ground. Tilopa's gurus are yogins, Tilopa becomes one, and the first of Tilopa's tamings is of a yogin.

### yojana

དཔག་ཚད *dpag tshad*; Sanskrit yojana.

An Indian measure of a long distance, the way a yoked team walks in a day, reckoned in different books at between about four and nine miles. When 1.4.3 tells of a sal tree a yojana high, it means a tree past all measure.

### yoke's length

གཉའ་ཤིང *gnya' shing*.

As far ahead as a yoke reaches, the distance a monastic's eyes may look while walking. In part 3 Tilopa goes begging for food with eyes cast down no farther than a yoke's length ahead.

### Zahor

ཟ་ཧོར *za hor*; Sanskrit Sahor.

The land in the east of India where Tilopa is born, in the city of Dzako, and whose beings Tilopa sets on the path at the end.
